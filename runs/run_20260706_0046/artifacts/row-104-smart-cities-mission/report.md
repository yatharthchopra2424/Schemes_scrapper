## Scheme Deep Dive

### Overview
The **Smart Cities Mission** is a centrally sponsored scheme launched by the **Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs (MoHUA)** to promote sustainable and inclusive urban development across India. It operates as a **challenge-based grant program** where cities compete for central financial assistance through a competitive selection process. The mission aims to transform 100 cities into smart cities by leveraging technology, innovation, and citizen-centric planning to improve quality of life, infrastructure, and governance.

### Objectives
The mission seeks to achieve the following objectives:
- Promote cities that provide core infrastructure and a decent quality of life to citizens.
- Apply smart solutions to infrastructure and services in area-based development.
- Use technology, information, and data to improve infrastructure and services.
- Ensure sustainable and inclusive development with a focus on the poor and disadvantaged.
- Strengthen municipal finance and improve urban governance.
- Encourage private sector participation through PPPs and other innovative financing models.

### Geographic Scope & Implementing Agency
- **Geographic Scope**: Pan-India  
- **Implementing Agency**: Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs (MoHUA)  
- **Application Portal**: [https://smartcities.gov.in](https://smartcities.gov.in)  
- **Contact Details**:  
  - Email: smartcities@mohua.gov.in  
  - Helpline: 1800-11-4477  

### Fund Size & Financial Support
- **Total Fund Size**: INR 20,000 crore (for 100 cities)  
- **Maximum Central Assistance per City**: INR 100 crore  
- **Matching Requirement**: Equal contribution from State Government and Urban Local Body (ULB)  
- **Total Project Cost per City**: INR 200 crore (INR 100 crore central + INR 100 crore state/ULB)  
- **Disbursement Schedule**:  
  - INR 50 crore in the first year  
  - INR 10 crore each year for the next four years (Years 2–5)  
- **Release Mechanism**: Funds are released in installments based on achievement of predefined milestones and submission of utilization certificates (UCs).  

> **Warning**: Central assistance will **not** be released unless matched by an equal contribution from the state government and ULB. Failure to submit utilization certificates on time will result in suspension of further fund releases.

### Eligibility Criteria
Eligibility is determined through a **competitive challenge process**. States nominate cities based on predefined criteria. Only nominated cities can proceed to prepare a Smart City Proposal (SCP).

| Criterion | Requirement | Details |
|---------|-------------|---------|
| **Nominating Authority** | State Government | States must nominate cities for participation in the Smart Cities Challenge. |
| **Urban Population** | Minimum threshold | Cities must meet a minimum urban population threshold (as defined per challenge round). |
| **Municipal Corporation** | Presence required | Only cities with a municipal corporation or equivalent ULB are eligible. |
| **Readiness for Reform** | Demonstrated commitment | Cities must show willingness and capacity to undertake urban reforms (e.g., taxation, user charges, GIS-based property taxation). |
| **Smart City Proposal (SCP)** | Mandatory | Nominated cities must prepare a detailed SCP covering vision, area-based development, pan-city initiatives, and financial sustainability. |
| **Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV)** | Required post-selection | Selected cities must incorporate an SPV under the Companies Act, 2013 for mission implementation. |

> **Note**: There is **no rolling deadline**. Selection occurs through periodic challenge rounds with timelines announced for each round. Cities must wait for the next challenge round if not selected in the current cycle.

### Target Beneficiaries
- Urban Local Bodies (ULBs)  
- State Governments  
- Municipal Corporations  

### Benefits
Selected cities receive:
- **Central Financial Assistance**: INR 100 crore over five years (INR 50 crore in Year 1, INR 10 crore/year for Years 2–5).  
- **Matched Funding**: Equal contribution (INR 100 crore) from state government and ULB, bringing total project funding to INR 200 crore per city.  
- **Non-Financial Benefits**:  
  - Improved urban infrastructure (water supply, sanitation, sewerage, stormwater drainage).  
  - Enhanced mobility (intelligent traffic management, pedestrian pathways, non-motorized transport).  
  - Better service delivery through technology (IoT, data analytics, smart grids).  
  - Increased investment and job creation.  
  - Improved quality of life, especially for poor and disadvantaged communities.  
  - Strengthened municipal finance and governance.  
  - Greater private sector participation via PPPs.  

### Required Documents
The following documents are mandatory at various stages of the mission:

| Document | Purpose | When Required |
|--------|--------|---------------|
| Smart City Proposal (SCP) | Outlines vision, area-based development, pan-city initiatives, and financial plan | During challenge application (Stage 2) |
| Certificate of Incorporation of SPV | Legal proof of SPV formation | After selection (Stage 4) |
| Board Resolution of SPV | Authorizes SPV to undertake mission activities | After SPV incorporation |
| Utilization Certificates (UCs) | Proof of fund utilization for release of next installment | Before each fund tranche release |
| Project Reports & Progress Updates | Tracks implementation against milestones | Periodically during implementation |
| Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) | Between state, ULB, and SPV on roles, responsibilities, and funding | Post-selection, pre-implementation |

### Application Process
The selection and implementation process follows a structured, challenge-based approach:

```mermaid
flowchart TD
    A[State Nominates Cities] --> B[Nominated Cities Prepare Smart City Proposal (SCP)]
    B --> C[SCPs Evaluated by National-Level Committee]
    C --> D[Selected Cities Announced via Challenge Round]
    D --> E[Selected City Incorporates Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV)]
    E --> F[SPV Signs MoU with State & ULB]
    F --> G[SPV Implements Projects as per SCP]
    G --> H[Submit Progress Reports & Utilization Certificates]
    H --> I{Achieved Milestones?}
    I -->|Yes| J[Release of Next Fund Installment]
    I -->|No| K[Withhold Funds; Issue Show Cause Notice]
    J --> L[Repeat H–I for Next Tranche]
    K --> L
    L --> M[Completion of 5-Year Mission Period]
    M --> N[Project Closure & Final UC Submission]
```

**Process Steps Explained**:
1. **Nomination**: States nominate eligible cities based on urban population, municipal corporation status, and reform readiness.
2. **Proposal Preparation**: Nominated cities develop a comprehensive Smart City Proposal (SCP) detailing vision, area-based development (ABD), pan-city initiatives, and financial sustainability.
3. **Evaluation**: SCPs are evaluated by a national-level committee through a competitive challenge process. Evaluation criteria include vision, feasibility, innovation, citizen participation, and financial plan.
4. **Selection**: Winners are announced per challenge round. No rolling admissions; cities must wait for the next round if not selected.
5. **SPV Incorporation**: Selected cities must incorporate a Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV) under the Companies Act, 2013 to implement the mission.
6. **MoU Execution**: A tripartite Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) is signed between the state government, ULB, and SPV outlining roles, responsibilities, and funding commitments.
7. **Implementation**: The SPV executes projects as per the SCP, focusing on area-based development (e.g., retrofitting, redevelopment, greenfield) and pan-city initiatives (e.g., smart traffic, water management).
8. **Monitoring & Reporting**: SPVs submit quarterly progress reports and utilization certificates (UCs) to MoHUA.
9. **Fund Release**: Central funds are released in installments (INR 50 crore Year 1, INR 10 crore/year Years 2–5) **only** upon achievement of milestones and valid UC submission.
10. **Completion**: At the end of the five-year mission period, final project closure occurs after submission of final UCs and impact assessment.

### Key Caveats
> - Funds are released **strictly** upon achievement of predefined milestones and submission of utilization certificates. Delays in UC submission will delay in further
> - Central assistance of INR 100 crore **must** by an equal contribution (100 crore)** per city **must be matched** by an equal contribution (INR 100 crore) from the state government and ULB. Failure to arrange matching funds will result in forfeiture of central assistance.
> - Selection is **highly competitive** and based solely on the quality of the Smart City Proposal (SCP). Cities are advised to engage expert consultants for SCP preparation.
> - The mission period is **limited to five years** from the date of selection. Funds must be utilized within this timeframe; unutilized funds may be recalled.
> - All financial transactions must be routed through the SPV. ULBs and state governments cannot directly access mission funds.
> - The SPV must comply with statutory audit requirements and submit audited annual accounts to MoHUA.
