# Comprehensive Scheme Masterclass & File Guide

## Scheme Deep Dive

### Overview
The National Mission for Sustainable Agriculture (NMSA) is a centrally sponsored scheme implemented by the Department of Agriculture & Cooperation, Ministry of Agriculture, Government of India. It operates on a pan-India geographic scope and is classified as a subsidy-type scheme. The scheme was last updated in 2014 and remains active with annual action plan (AAP) submissions required yearly. Funds are released based on AAP approval and utilization of previous funds, with revalidation of unspent funds permitted subject to conditions.

### Objectives
NMSA aims to:
- Make agriculture more productive, sustainable, remunerative, and climate-resilient by promoting location-specific integrated/composite farming systems.
- Conserve natural resources through appropriate soil and moisture conservation measures.
- Adopt comprehensive soil health management practices based on soil fertility maps, soil test-based application of macro & micro nutrients, and judicious use of fertilizers.
- Optimize utilization of water resources through efficient water management to expand coverage for achieving 'more crop per drop'.
- Develop capacity of farmers & stakeholders in climate change adaptation and mitigation measures, in conjunction with ongoing missions like NMAE&T, NFSM, and NICRA.
- Pilot models in select blocks for improving productivity of rainfed farming by mainstreaming rainfed technologies refined through NICRA and leveraging resources from other schemes like MGNREGS, IWMP, and RKVY.
- Establish effective inter and intra-departmental/ministerial coordination for accomplishing key deliverables under the National Action Plan on Climate Change (NAPCC).

### Application Process
The application process for NMSA involves State Governments preparing Annual Action Plans (AAPs) based on fund availability and past performance, submitting them to the Government of India for approval by the Project Sanctioning Committee, and receiving funds in installments upon submission of Utilization Certificates and progress reports. States implement components through district and village-level agencies, with beneficiaries accessing benefits via State-level implementing departments/agencies. Monitoring and evaluation are conducted quarterly by technical personnel and reported to the National Advisory Committee.

#### Key Process Details
- **AAP Submission Deadline**: Latest by March end for examination, deliberation, and final approval (NMSA Guidelines, p. 18)
- **Fund Release Contingent Upon**: Submission of Utilization Certificates and detailed progress reports (Key Facts, NMSA Guidelines p. 20)
- **Unspent Funds Revalidation**: Permitted if adjusted against current year's first/second installment (Key Facts)
- **Interest/Earnings**: Must be remitted to Consolidated Fund of India (Key Facts)
- **AAP Requirements**: Must include unfinished/spill-over activities and committed liabilities from previous year (Key Facts)
- **Progress Reporting**: 
  - Quarterly Progress Reports (QPR) due by 10th of first month of next quarter (NMSA Guidelines p. 20)
  - Annual Progress Report (APR) due within three months after financial year closure (NMSA Guidelines p. 20)
  - Detailed APR format in Appendix-II A (NMSA Guidelines p. 45-46)
  - TSP/SCSP reporting format in Appendix-IV (NMSA Guidelines p. 54-55)
- **Monitoring System**: 
  - Three-tier system: National (PSC/STC), State (SSTC/SMSA/SLC), District (DMC) (NMSA Guidelines p. 8-11)
  - Web-based monitoring, video conferencing, desk reviews, field visits (NMSA Guidelines p. 19)
  - Digital location of project areas uploaded on Bhuvan Platform (SMAF Guidelines p. 11)
  - Quarterly/Annual Progress Reports submitted as per Annexure-VI formats (SMAF Guidelines p. 11)
  - Monthly/quarterly progress reports updated within 15 days of month/quarter end (SMAF Guidelines p. 11)
  - Detailed APR submitted by 30th May of next financial year; MIS database frozen 3 months after financial year end (SMAF Guidelines p. 11)

### Key Caveats & Critical Notes
> **Funding Pattern Variability**: 60:40 for general states, 90:10 for NE & hilly states, 100% for UTs and National Level Agencies (Key Facts, SMAF Guidelines p. 12)
> 
> **Fund Release Contingency**: Release of funds is contingent upon submission of Utilization Certificates and progress reports (Key Facts, NMSA Guidelines p. 20)
> 
> **Unspent Funds Revalidation**: Unspent balances from previous financial year are revalidated only if adjusted against current year's first/second installment (Key Facts)
> 
> **Interest Remittance**: Interest or earnings against grants-in-aid must be remitted to the Consolidated Fund of India (Key Facts)
> 
> **AAP Completeness**: AAP must include unfinished/spill-over activities and committed liabilities from previous year (Key Facts)
> 
> **Beneficiary Targeting**: At least 50% of allocation for small/marginal farmers; of which at least 30% are women beneficiaries/farmers (NMSA Guidelines p. 20, SMAF Guidelines p. 12)
> 
> **Special Component Plans**: 16% & 8% of total allocation (or SC/ST population proportion) for SCP and TSP respectively (NMSA Guidelines p. 20, SMAF Guidelines p. 12)
> 
> **Transit Rules Precondition**: For SMAF specifically, liberal transit rules for timber transport are a precondition for availing benefits (SMAF Guidelines p. 12, State-wise list PDF)
> 
> **Soil Health Card Prerequisite**: Made a pre-requisite for farmers getting benefits under SMAF (SMAF Guidelines p. 4, 7)
> 
> **NGO Engagement**: States may engage reputed NGOs for cluster/village development in limited govt. infrastructure areas via transparent selection and supervision (NMSA Guidelines p. 6, 11)
> 
> **Consultant Engagement**: Provision for National (8), State (84 max 3 per state), and District (300) consultants under NMSA Guidelines Appendix-V (p. 55-58)
> 
> **Honorarium Rates**: 
> - National Advisors/Chief Consultants: Rs. 70,000/month (NMSA Guidelines Appendix-V(A) p. 58)
> - State Advisors/Consultants: Rs. 50,000/month (p. 58)
> - District Advisors/Consultants: Rs. 30,000/month (p. 58)
> - Technical Assistants: National Rs. 30,000, State Rs. 25,000, District Rs. 20,000 (p. 58)

### Expected Outcomes & Impact
- Transform Indian agriculture into more climate-resilient production systems through adaptation and mitigation measures
- Absorb improved technology and best practices; promote coping mechanisms for climatic and non-climatic stresses
- Expand coverage primarily to rainfed areas by integrating farming systems with livestock and fisheries
- Conserve and sustainably use natural resources for food and livelihood security
- Additional income/savings opportunities for farmers through agroforestry (SMAF Guidelines p. 13)
- Increased tree cover leading to higher carbon sequestration (SMAF Guidelines p. 13)
- Enrichment of soil organic matter and enhanced nutrient uptake (SMAF Guidelines p. 13)
- Availability of agroforestry produce to save foreign exchange on wood imports (SMAF Guidelines p. 13)
- Development of information system and database on agroforestry (SMAF Guidelines p. 13)
- Income and livelihood generation for farmers (SMAF Guidelines p. 13)
- Quantifiable success indicators: Number of plants, soil carbon sequestration, soil organic matter, livelihood improvement, productivity enhancement (SMAF Guidelines p. 11)
