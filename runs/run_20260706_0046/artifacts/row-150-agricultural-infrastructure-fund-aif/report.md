# Comprehensive Scheme Masterclass & File Guide

## Scheme Deep Dive

### Overview
The Agriculture Infrastructure Fund (AIF) is a Central Sector Scheme launched by the Government of India under the Atmanirbhar Bharat Package in May 2020. It provides a financing facility of ₹1 lakh crore for investment in post-harvest management infrastructure and viable farming assets at farm-gate and aggregation points. The scheme is operational from 2020-21 to 2032-33, with loan disbursement to be completed by the end of Financial Year 2025-26. As of 30th June 2024, ₹43,391 crores have been sanctioned, of which ₹28,171 crores have been disbursed. The implementing agency is the Department of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare (DA&FW), Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare, Government of India. The application portal is https://agriinfra.dac.gov.in. Applications are accepted on a rolling basis with no fixed deadline.

### Objectives
The AIF scheme fulfills numerous objectives for all stakeholders in the agriculture ecosystem:

- **Farmers (including FPOs, PACS, Marketing Cooperative Societies, Multipurpose cooperative societies, State Agencies, Agricultural Produce Market Committees (Mandis), National & State Federations of Cooperatives, Federations of FPOs and Federations of Self Help Groups (SHGs))**:
  - Improved marketing infrastructure to allow farmers to sell directly to a larger base of consumers and increase value realization.
  - With investments in logistics infrastructure, farmers will be able to sell in the market with reduced post-harvest losses and a smaller number of intermediaries.
  - With modern packaging and cold storage system access, farmers will be able to further decide when to sell in the market and improve realization.
  - Viable farming assets for improved productivity and optimization of inputs will result in substantial savings to farmers.

- **Government**:
  - Government will be able to direct priority sector lending in currently unviable projects by supporting through interest subvention, incentive through convergence, and credit guarantee.
  - Due to improvements in post-harvest infrastructure, government will further be able to reduce national food wastage percentage, enabling the agriculture sector to become competitive with current global levels.
  - Central/State Government Agencies or local bodies will be able to structure viable projects on their own or PPP projects for attracting investment in agriculture infrastructure.

- **Agri-entrepreneurs and startups**:
  - With a dedicated source of funding, entrepreneurs will push for innovation in the agriculture sector by leveraging new age technologies including IoT, AI, etc.
  - It will also connect the players in the ecosystem and improve avenues for collaboration between entrepreneurs and farmers.

- **Banking ecosystem**:
  - With Credit Guarantee, convergence, and interest subvention, lending institutions will be able to lend with lower risk.
  - This scheme will help to enlarge their customer base and diversification of portfolio.
  - Refinance facility will enable a larger role for cooperative banks and RRBs.

- **Consumers**:
  - With reduced inefficiencies in the post-harvest ecosystem, key benefit for consumers will be a larger share of produce reaching the market and hence, better quality and prices.
  - Overall, the investment via the financing facility in agriculture infrastructure will benefit all stakeholders in the eco-system.

### Eligibility Matrix
**Eligible Beneficiaries**:
- Primary Agricultural Credit Societies (PACS)
- Marketing Cooperative Societies
- Farmer Producer Organizations (FPOs)
- Self Help Groups (SHGs)
- Farmers
- Joint Liability Groups (JLGs)
- Multipurpose Cooperative Societies
- Agri-entrepreneurs
- Startups
- Central/State agency or Local Body sponsored Public Private Partnership Projects
- State Agencies
- Agricultural Produce Market Committees (Mandis)
- National & State Federations of Cooperatives
- Federations of FPOs
- Federations of Self Help Groups (SHGs)

**Target Beneficiaries**: farmers; FPOs; agri-entrepreneurs; startups; PACS; SHGs; JLGs; multipurpose cooperative societies; marketing cooperative societies; state agencies

**Ineligible**:
- Standalone secondary processing infrastructures are not eligible (Note 3).
- Not eligible for individuals/farmers for certain infrastructure components (Note 4).
- Component-A of PM-KUSUM is eligible for Individual farmers/Group of farmers/FPOs/Cooperatives/Panchayats to set up solar power plants of capacity up to 2 MW on barren/cultivable/uncultivable/pasturelands or marshy lands (Note 5).

**Number of Projects per Entity**:
- Interest subvention for a loan up to ₹2 crore in one location is eligible under the scheme.
- Multiple projects in one location are also eligible with an overall cap of ₹2 crore.
- If one eligible entity puts up projects in different locations, all such projects will be eligible under the scheme for loan up to ₹2 crore.
- However, for a private sector entity (such as farmer, agri entrepreneur, start-up), there will be a limit of maximum of 25 such projects.
- This limitation of 25 projects will not be applicable to state agencies, cooperatives, national and state federations of cooperatives, FPOs, federations of FPOs, SHGs, and federation of SHGs.
- Location means physical boundary of a village or town having a distinct LGD (Local Government Directory) code. Each project should be in a location having a separate LGD code.
- For APMCs, multiple projects of different infrastructure types can be sanctioned in its designated market area. In such cases, interest subvention for a loan up to ₹2 Crore will be provided for each project of different infrastructure types (e.g., cold storage, sorting, grading, assaying units, silos) within the designated market area of the APMC.

### Benefits & Financial Support
**Financial Benefits**:
- Interest subvention of 3% per annum up to a limit of ₹2 crore per project, available for a maximum period of 7 years.
  - For loans beyond ₹2 crore, interest subvention will be limited to ₹2 crore.
- Credit guarantee coverage available for loans up to ₹2 crore under the CGTMSE scheme, with the government bearing the fee.
  - For FPOs, credit guarantee may be availed from the facility created under FPO promotion scheme of DA&FW and NABSanrakshan Trustee Company Pvt. Ltd.
  - FPOs are also eligible for reimbursement of credit guarantee fee under AIF.
- For loans taken by Primary Agricultural Credit Societies (PACS) under AIF for warehouses/godowns construction projects, Credit Guarantee coverage is extended from (2+5) years to (2+8) years (applicable to prospective projects only from the date of issue of Order No.101 010112024-AlF dated 24.09.2025).

**Non-Financial Benefits**:
- Convergence with various Central and State Government schemes (MIDH, PMFME, SMAM, GOBAR-Dhan, PMKSY, AMI, PACS as MSC, RKVY, PMEGP, and PM-KUSUM (Components A, B & C)) for selected eligible projects/assets and beneficiaries as per AIF Scheme Guidelines.
- Online single window facility in collaboration with participating lending institutions.
- Project Management Unit (PMU) support for handholding in project preparation.

**Size of Financing Facility**: ₹1 lakh crore (₹100,000 crore).

**Repayment Period**: Maximum of 7 years including a moratorium period of up to 2 years.

**Cap on Lending Rate**: Lending rate of participating lending entities will be decided after due consultation with lending entities and circulated to all stakeholders. Lending institutions will sign MOU with DA&FW/NABARD for implementation of the Scheme, with a view to negotiating cap on lending rates in a fair manner.

**Refinance**: Need-based refinance support will be made available by NABARD to all eligible lending entities including cooperative banks and RRBs as per its policy.

### Application Process
The application process involves the following steps:
1. Visit the AIF portal at https://agriinfra.dac.gov.in.
2. Register as a beneficiary through the Beneficiary Registration section.
3. Prepare a Detailed Project Report (DPR) using the template provided.
4. Approach a participating lending institution (Commercial Banks, Cooperative Banks, RRBs, Small Finance Banks, NCDC, NBFCs, etc.) with the DPR.
5. The lending institution processes the loan application under the AIF scheme.
6. Upon sanction, the loan is disbursed by the lending institution.
7. Interest subvention and credit guarantee claims are submitted through the AIF portal.

**Required Documents**:
1. Bank’s loan application form / Customer Request Letter for AIF Loan duly filled and signed
2. Passport size photographs of the promoter/partners/director
3. Identity proof – Voter ID card/PAN card/Aadhaar card/Driving license
4. Address Proof: Residence: Voter ID card/Passport/Aadhaar card/Driving license/Electricity Bill/Latest property Tax Bill
5. Business Office/Registered Office: Electricity Bill/Latest Property Tax Receipt/Certificate of Incorporation in case of Companies/Certificate of Registration in case of partnership Firms
6. Proof of Registration: In case of Company: Article of Association; In case of Partnership: Certificate of Registration of Firm with Registrar of Firm; In case of MSMEs: Certificate of Registration with District Industries Centre (DIC)/Udyog Aadhar
7. Income Tax Return for last three years, If available
8. Audited Balance Sheet of last 3 Years, If available
9. GST Certificate, if applicable
10. Land ownership / lease records
11. ROC Search Report of the Company
12. KYC documents of the promoter/firm/company
13. Copy of Bank Statement for last one year (If available)
14. Repayment track record of existing loans (Loan Statement)
15. Net Worth Statements of promoter
16. Detailed Project Report
17. As applicable: Local authority permissions, Layout plans/estimates, Building sanction

**Key Caveats**:
- Interest subvention of 3% p.a. is limited to ₹2 crore per project in one location, though loan amount can be higher.
- For a private sector entity (such as farmer, agri entrepreneur, start-up), there will be a limit of maximum of 25 such projects.
- Limitation of 25 projects will not be applicable to state agencies, national and state federations of cooperatives, federations of FPOs, and federation of SHGs.
- Standalone secondary processing infrastructures are not eligible.
- Not eligible for individuals/farmers for certain infrastructure components (Note 4).
- Component-A of PM-KUSUM is eligible for Individual farmers/Group of farmers/FPOs/Cooperatives/Panchayats to set up solar power plants of capacity up to 2 MW on barren/cultivable/uncultivable/pasturelands or marshy lands (Note 5).

### Participating Lending Institutions
After signing of Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) with National Bank for Agriculture & Rural Development (NABARD)/DA&FW, the following eligible lending institutions may participate:
- All scheduled Commercial Banks
- Scheduled Cooperative Banks
- Regional Rural Banks (RRBs)
- Small Finance Banks
- Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs)
- National Cooperative Development Corporation (NCDC)
- DCCBs with PACS affiliation

### Monitoring Framework
The National, State, and District Level Monitoring Committees ensure real-time monitoring and effective feedback about the implementation of the proposed scheme.

**National Level Monitoring Committee (NLMC)**:
- Composition: Secretary (DA&FW) (Chairman), MD SFAC, MD, NCDC, Special Secretary/Additional Secretary and FA (DA&FW), Additional Secretary DFS, Additional Secretary (DA&FW, GoI), Chairman, NABARD or his representative, Principal Secretary-State Government (s) - Four States by rotation, State Nodal Officers of four States (by rotation), Joint Secretary (DA&FW) and CEO of Farmers Welfare Programme Implementation Society (Member Secretary).
- Functions: Guide and steer the implementation of the scheme; approve guidelines for implementation; National Level Implementation Committee (NLIC) examines and recommends guidelines for implementation and ensures review of implementation as per approved guidelines by NLMC.

**State Level Monitoring Committee (SLMC)**:
- Composition: Chief Secretary – Chairman, Agriculture Production Commissioner/Principal Secretary Agriculture, Principal Secretary (Cooperation), Registrar of Cooperative Societies (RCS), Chief General Manager (CGM), NABARD, Regional Director, NCDC, Officers nominated by State (not more than three), SLBC Convener, State Nodal Officer - Member Secretary.
- Functions: Implement NIMC guidelines at the state level and provide feedback to NIMC; guide and steer implementation of the scheme in the state; set targets as per OOMF format and review progress regularly.

**District Level Monitoring Committee (DLMC)**:
- Composition: District Collector – Chairman, Chief Executive Officer of District Panchayat/CDO - Vice Chairman, District Officer of Agriculture, District Registrar Officers nominated Cooperative Societies, Officers nominated by State (not more than three), Lead District Manager of DLBC, District Manager NABARD - Member Secretary.
- Functions: First line of implementation and monitoring system within the overall framework; set targets in consultation with SLMC as per OOMF format and monitor progress closely with PMU support; maintain Dashboard in collaboration with PMU; responsible for smooth implementation and resolution of issues at district level with support from district administration.

**Project Management Units (PMUs)**:
- Farmers Welfare Programme Implementation Society under DA&FW provides PMU support at the central level.
- State PMUs of PM KISAN at state level provide handholding support for implementation of the scheme.
- PMUs provide handholding support for implementation, monitor progress closely with DLMC support, and maintain Dashboard in collaboration with DLMC.

**Output and Outcome Monitoring Framework (OOMF)**:
- Part of the monitoring system; measurement of outcome indicators monitored periodically by DLMC, SLMC, and NLMC.
- Interest subvention and credit guarantee support released to Banks and lending institutions through PFMS.
- Disbursal of funds by lending entities to beneficiaries under this scheme shall be in Aadhaar-linked bank account.
- All assets created under this financing facility shall be geo-tagged. District Monitoring Committee and respective lending entity ensure updated information on geo-tagged assets is available on the online portal.
