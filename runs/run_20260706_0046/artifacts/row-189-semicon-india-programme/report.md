## Scheme Deep Dive

### Overview
The **Semicon India Programme** is a pan-India subsidy scheme administered by the **India Semiconductor Mission (ISM)** under the **Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY)**. Launched with a total fund size of **₹76,000 Crore**, the programme aims to build a comprehensive semiconductor ecosystem in India by providing financial incentives across the value chain—from fabrication and design to assembly, testing, marking, and packaging (ATMP/OSAT). Applications are accepted periodically through notification windows announced by ISM via the official portal: [https://semicon.gov.in](https://semicon.gov.in). The scheme was last updated in 2024 and remains active with high confidence in data accuracy.

### Objectives
The Semicon India Programme seeks to:
- Provide financial support to companies investing in semiconductor, display, and design ecosystems  
- Reduce reliance on imports and enhance domestic value addition in electronics manufacturing  
- Create employment opportunities and develop skilled manpower in advanced technology sectors  
- Encourage innovation and R&D in semiconductor technologies and applications  
- Attract significant investments in semiconductor manufacturing and related facilities  
- Strengthen India's position in the global semiconductor supply chain  
- Develop a complete ecosystem supporting semiconductor manufacturing from design to fabrication and testing

### Application Process Flow
The application process is structured and sequential, involving multiple stages of scrutiny and approval. Below is a Mermaid flowchart illustrating the end-to-end journey:

```mermaid
flowchart TD
    A[Identify Relevant Scheme] --> B[Prepare Detailed Project Report (DPR)]
    B --> C[Submit Application Online via https://semicon.gov.in]
    C --> D[Preliminary Scrutiny for Completeness & Eligibility]
    D --> E{Eligible & Complete?}
    E -->|No| F[Application Rejected / Resubmit]
    E -->|Yes| G[Technical Evaluation by Expert Committee]
    G --> H[Financial Appraisal & Due Diligence]
    H --> I[Recommendation by Inter-Ministerial Committee]
    I --> J[Final Approval by Union Cabinet / Designated Authority]
    J --> K[Signing of MoU/Agreement with ISM]
    K --> L[Disbursement in Tranches Based on Milestone Achievement & Expenditure Verification]
    L --> M[Project Implementation & Monitoring]
    M --> N[Continued Disbursement Subject to Milestone Compliance]
    N --> O[Project Completion & Closure]
    style A fill:#e3f2fd,stroke:#1565c0
    style L fill:#fff3e0,stroke:#ef6c00
    style O fill:#e8f5e8,stroke:#2e7d32
```

> **Key Points**:
> - Applications are accepted only during notified windows; no rolling admissions.
> - The **Detailed Project Report (DPR)** must include technical feasibility, financial viability, land acquisition, water/power needs, environmental clearance status, and employment generation.
> - Post-approval, disbursement is **strictly tied to milestone achievement** and requires **verification of expenditure**.
> - Land must be acquired or possessed within specified timelines; environmental and statutory clearances are mandatory.

### Required Documents
Applicants must submit the following documents as part of their application:

1. Certificate of Incorporation/Registration  
2. Memorandum and Articles of Association / Partnership Deed / LLP Agreement  
3. Board Resolution authorizing the application  
4. Detailed Project Report (DPR)  
5. Land documents (title deed, lease agreement, or allotment letter)  
6. Projected financial statements for 5–10 years  
7. Details of promoters and directors/partners  
8. Technical details of proposed technology/process  
9. Environmental clearance documents (if applicable)  
10. Clearances from relevant authorities (pollution control, fire safety, etc.)  
11. Bank appraisal report or letter of sanction for debt portion  
12. Undertaking regarding compliance with scheme guidelines  
13. For DLI Scheme: details of semiconductor design IP, proposed products, and deployment plans  

> **Note**: Incomplete documentation leads to rejection at the preliminary scrutiny stage.
