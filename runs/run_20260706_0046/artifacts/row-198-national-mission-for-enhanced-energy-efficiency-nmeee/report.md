# Comprehensive Scheme Masterclass & File Guide

## Scheme Deep Dive

### Overview
The National Mission for Enhanced Energy Efficiency (NMEEE) is one of the eight national missions under the National Action Plan on Climate Change (NAPCC). Its primary objective is to develop regulations and policies that strengthen the market for energy efficiency through self-regulation and market principles, capacity building, and financing initiatives. The scheme operates on a pan-India geographic scope with rolling basis application deadlines and is administered by the Bureau of Energy Efficiency (BEE), Ministry of Power, Government of India. The application portal is https://beeindia.gov.in/, last updated in 2026.

### Objectives
- Develop regulations and policies to strengthen the market for energy efficiency  
- Promote energy efficiency through self-regulation and market principles  
- Implement capacity building and financing initiatives for energy efficiency  
- Achieve required energy efficiency in energy intensive sectors via PAT scheme  
- Promote awareness of energy savings and energy conservation among targeted groups  
- Leverage multi-lateral, bi-lateral and private sector support for programme implementation  
- Establish systems to measure, monitor and verify energy efficiency results at sector and national levels  
- Demonstrate energy efficiency delivery mechanisms through Public-Private Partnership (PPP)  

### Eligibility Matrix
Eligibility varies significantly across sub-schemes under NMEEE. The table below summarizes key eligibility criteria based on evidence.

| Sub-Scheme | Target Beneficiaries | Eligibility Criteria |
|------------|----------------------|----------------------|
| Perform, Achieve and Trade (PAT) | Energy intensive units from notified sectors | Designated Consumers (DCs) notified by Central Government based on specific energy consumption norms in sectors: Aluminum, Cement, Chlor-Alkali, Fertilizer, Iron & Steel, Paper & Pulp, Railways, Thermal Power, Textile, Petrochemicals, Commercial Buildings (Hotels), Airports, DISCOMs, Refineries |
| SME Programmes | MSMEs | Eligibility based on MSME classification as per MSME Act |
| Standards & Labelling | Manufacturers of appliances and equipment | Can apply for star rating certification through BEE web portal |
| Energy Conservation Building Code (ECBC) | New commercial buildings | Applicable to buildings with connected load of 100 kW or more, or contract demand of 120 kVA or more |
| State Designated Agencies (SDAs) | State/UT Governments | Must be designated by State Government/UT Administration under Section 15(d) of EC Act, 2001 |
| Energy Service Companies (ESCOs) | Private entities | Must be empaneled with BEE; validity typically till June 30th (as per 2020 list) |
| ECBC Expert Professionals | Individuals/Firms | Must be empaneled with BEE; listed in alphabetical order with contact details |

**Key Financial Mechanisms:**
- **Perform, Achieve and Trade (PAT) Scheme**: Market-based mechanism where excess energy savings are converted into tradable Energy Savings Certificates (ESCerts). DCs exceeding norms receive ESCerts; those falling short must purchase them to comply.
- **Financing Energy Efficiency Programme (FEEP)**: Umbrella programme covering Energy Efficiency Financing Platform (EEFP) and Framework for Energy Efficient Economic Development (FEEED).
- **Energy Efficiency Financing Platform (EEFP)**: Platform for Financial Institutions (FIs) to interact with industries; includes "Investment Bazaar" events (e.g., Vizag and Chandigarh in 2021 showcasing >Rs. 11 crore of EE projects).
- **Framework for Energy Efficient Economic Development (FEEED)**: Includes Partial Risk Guarantee Fund for Energy Efficiency (PRGFEE) and Venture Capital Fund for Energy Efficiency (VCFEE); existing programmes: Energy Efficiency Financing Facility (EEFF) anchored by PFC and IREDA; Facilitation Centre for BEE’s financing schemes.
- **Grading of Energy Efficiency Projects**: Launched July 2021; reimburses grading fee for graded and financed EE projects; empaneled agencies: CARE Advisory and Research Training Ltd (CART), CRISIL Ltd, ICRA Analytics Ltd; participating FIs: IREDA and YES Bank.
- **State Energy Conservation Fund (SECF)**: Section 16(1) of EC Act 2001; BEE contributes up to Rs. 4.0 crore per State/UT in two installments of Rs. 2.0 crore each (matching contribution relaxed to Rs. 25 lakh for NE States/UTs); funds used for Revolving Investment Fund (RIF) to finance EE projects in public buildings, street lighting, water pumping, MSME units, etc.
- **Strengthening of State Designated Agencies (SDAs)**: Scheme continued from FY 2021-22 to FY 2025-26; includes State Partnership for Energy Efficiency Demonstrations (SPEED), Model Energy Efficient Village Campaign, Institutionalization of enforcement machinery, Manpower support, State Energy Efficiency Research & Outreach Programme, Workshops/capacity building of energy professionals, Maintenance of internet platform, Student Awareness/Student Capacity Building Programme (SCBP), Contribution to SECF.

**Key Process Notes from Evidence:**
- **PAT**: Designated Consumers are notified by Central Government based on energy consumption norms; must comply with notified norms, rules, and regulations; involves baseline and target year Specific Energy Consumption (SEC) assessment; excess savings converted to ESCerts tradable on power exchanges.
- **Standards & Labelling**: Manufacturers file applications online through BEE web portal for star labelling of appliances; BEE Star Label mobile app enables real-time feedback and comparison.
- **SME Programmes**: Interested entities apply through designated channels as per specific programme guidelines; includes energy and resource mapping in 40 SME clusters across 8 sectors (~400 detailed energy audits conducted).
- **ECBC**: Implementation at state and local level through integration in municipal bye-laws; 20 States and 2 UTs notified ECBC as of April 2021; 270 Urban Local Bodies (ULBs) from 128 States incorporated ECBC provisions.
- **General**: Application portal is https://beeindia.gov.in/; rolling basis status; contact details: Email: dg-bee[at]nic[dot]in | Phone: 011-26766702.

### Key Caveats & Critical Notes
> **Eligibility and benefits vary significantly across the different sub-schemes under NMEEE (PAT, SME, Standards & Labelling, ECBC, etc.)** – Consultants must verify the specific sub-scheme relevance before proceeding.
> 
> **Implementation of certain components like ECBC depends on state and local government adoption and enforcement** – ECBC compliance is not automatic; requires state/UT notification and municipal integration.
> 
> **Financial support mechanisms are subject to availability of funds and specific programme guidelines** – Financing access (e.g., EEFP, SECF) is not guaranteed and depends on budget allocations and matching contributions.
> 
> **The PAT scheme is a multi-cycle programme with each cycle of 3 years** – Clients must track cycle timelines (e.g., PAT Cycle VII: 2022-23 to 2024-25) and verification schedules.
> 
> **ESCerts trading is contingent on market mechanisms and power exchange participation** – Financial benefit from ESCerts depends on market prices and liquidity on power exchanges.
> 
> **State Designated Agencies (SDAs) must be formally designated** – Only 36 States/UTs have nominated SDAs; verification of SDA status is critical for state-level programme access.
> 
> **ECBC Expert Professionals and ESCOs require empaneled status** – Consultants must verify current empaneled lists (validity typically till June 30th annually) before recommending engagement.
