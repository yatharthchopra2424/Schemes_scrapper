## Stage 1: Eligibility Verification (Day 1–3)

### Checklist
- [ ] The applicant is a registered business entity in India (Private Limited Company, LLP, Partnership Firm, Proprietorship, or Society/Trust engaged in manufacturing)
- [ ] The applicant is engaged in defence manufacturing or related defence sector activities as defined under the Make in India for Defence (MII Defence) policy
- [ ] The applicant has a valid Udyam Registration (MSME) or is a large enterprise eligible under defence procurement guidelines
- [ ] The applicant’s annual turnover meets the threshold specified in the latest Defence Acquisition Procedure (DAP) or MII Defence guidelines (verify current threshold with nodal officer)
- [ ] The applicant has been in operation for a minimum period as stipulated in the current MII Defence framework (typically 3 years for established entities; relaxations may apply for startups under iDEX)
- [ ] The applicant is not blacklisted by the Ministry of Defence or any central government department
- [ ] The applicant complies with indigenous content requirements as per the latest Public Procurement (Preference to Make in India) Order, 2017 and its defence-specific amendments
- [ ] The applicant possesses necessary licences and clearances for defence manufacturing (e.g., Industrial Licence under IDR Act, NOC from MoD if applicable)
- [ ] The applicant is applying for benefits under a specific defence procurement project or scheme aligned with MII Defence objectives (e.g., SP model, Make-I, Make-II, or iDEX)

> **Note**: Eligibility criteria for MII Defence are primarily derived from the Defence Acquisition Procedure (DAP) 2020, Public Procurement (Preference to Make in India) Order, and specific project RFPs. Since the scheme operates as a policy framework rather than a centralized subsidy, eligibility must be verified against the specific tender or procurement notice under which benefits are sought.

## Stage 3: Application Drafting (Day 10–18)

### Project Report / DPR (if required)
✅ **Required** – A Detailed Project Report (DPR) or Technical Bid is mandatory for availing benefits under MII Defence, especially when responding to defence tenders under Make-I, Make-II, or iDEX routes.  

**Standard DPR Sections**:  
- Executive Summary  
- Project Objectives and Alignment with Atmanirbhar Bharat & MII Defence Goals  
- Technical Description of the Defence Equipment/System  
- Indigenous Design and Development Details (including % indigenous content)  
- Manufacturing Plan (facilities, technology transfer, production capacity)  
- Quality Assurance and Testing Protocol (DGQA, AQAP, MIL-STD compliance)  
- Project Timeline (Gantt chart)  
- Budget Breakdown (CAPEX, OPEX, funding sources)  
- Risk Assessment and Mitigation Plan  
- Compliance with Safety, Security, and Environmental Standards  
- Annexures: Drawings, Specifications, Test Reports, IPR details, Collaborator MoUs  

> **Note**: For innovation-focused routes (e.g., iDEX, DISC), a shorter proposal or proof-of-concept report may be acceptable. Verify requirements with the tender issuing authority.

### Application Form Filling Protocol
1. **Identify the correct procurement channel**: Determine if benefits are sought via Make-I (GoI-funded), Make-II (industry-funded), iDEX, or direct procurement under DPP-2020.  
2. **Use the official tender portal**: Apply only through the designated defence procurement portal (e.g., https://www.defenceprocurement.gov.in, GeM for defence, or MoD/DRDO e-tendering portals).  
3. **Fill indigenous content declaration accurately**: Use the format prescribed under PPP-MII Order; over-declaration may lead to penalties.  
4. **Attach all scheme-specific documents**: Ensure DPR, SIC, Industrial Licence, and MoD NOC are uploaded as per checklist.  
5. **Validate financial thresholds**: Confirm that turnover and net worth meet relaxation criteria (if claiming MSME or startup benefits).  
6. **Cross-check entity details**: Ensure name, PAN, GSTIN, and Udyam are identical across form and documents.  
7. **Sign and declare**: The authorized signatory must digitally sign the declaration of indigenous content and eligibility.  
8. **Save submission receipt**: Immediately download and store the acknowledgment slip with application number and timestamp.  

> **Portal Note**: Since MII Defence is policy-driven, there is no standalone application form. Benefits are claimed **within the bid submission** for defence tenders. Always refer to the specific tender document for procedural instructions.

## Stage 5: Post-Approval (Day 30–90+)

### Approval Actions
- [ ] Obtain and save the official approval letter or tender award notice indicating availing of MII Defence benefits (e.g., purchase preference clause applied)  
- [ ] Verify the sanctioned benefit (e.g., price preference percentage, relaxation in eligibility) matches the tender terms and PPP-MII Order  
- [ ] Initiate production or service delivery as per contract timeline  
- [ ] Maintain records of indigenous content utilization for audit and compliance  
- [ ] Prepare Utilization Certificate (UC) if required under any linked scheme (e.g., iDEX, DISC) – though pure MII Defence is a preference policy, not a grant  
- [ ] Track milestone payments and ensure invoices comply with contractual terms  
- [ ] Conduct internal success fee calculation as per engagement letter  
- [ ] File periodic compliance reports if mandated by the contracting authority (e.g., quarterly progress reports to MoD/DRDO)  
- [ ] Preserve all records (approval, UC, invoices, correspondence) for minimum 5 years for audit purposes  

> **Note**: MII Defence primarily confers procedural benefits (e.g., eligibility relaxation, purchase preference) in defence procurement. There is no direct fund disbursement; hence, "utilization certificate" applies only if linked to a concurrent grant scheme (e.g., iDEX). Focus on contract compliance and indigenous content verification.
