# Comprehensive Scheme Masterclass & File Guide

## Scheme Deep Dive

### Overview
The **National Urban Livelihood Mission (NULM)**, officially known as **Deendayal Antyodaya Yojana-National Urban Livelihoods Mission (DAY-NULM)**, is a flagship programme of the **Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs, Government of India**. It aims to reduce poverty and vulnerability of urban poor households by enabling access to self-employment and skilled wage employment opportunities. The scheme operates on a **rolling basis** with applications accepted throughout the year. The official application portal is [https://nulm.gov.in](https://nulm.gov.in).

### Objectives
The key objectives of DAY-NULM are:
- Reduce poverty and vulnerability of urban poor households by enabling access to self-employment and skilled wage employment opportunities.
- Build strong grassroots level institutions of the poor through social mobilisation and institution development.
- Provide shelter equipped with essential services to the urban homeless in a phased manner.
- Facilitate access to suitable spaces, institutional credit, social security, and skills for urban street vendors.
- Ensure universal coverage of urban poor for skill development and credit facilities.
- Promote convergence with schemes of relevant line ministries and state governments.
- Encourage partnership with private sector for skill training, employment, and shelter operations.
- Cater to especially vulnerable segments such as dependent children, aged, disabled, mentally ill, and recovering patients.

### Eligibility Matrix
The primary target is the **urban poor**, including the **urban homeless**. As an interim measure, the target is the urban population identified as below poverty line by States/UTs. Coverage may be broadened to include families of disadvantaged groups like SCs, STs, women, minorities, disabled, etc., subject to a maximum of 25 percent of the urban poor population.

| Beneficiary Category | Specific Eligibility Criteria |
|----------------------|-------------------------------|
| **Urban Poor** | Identified as below poverty line by States/UTs (interim measure) |
| **Urban Homeless** | Explicitly targeted under the scheme |
| **Street Vendors** | Eligible for identity cards, social security linkages, training, stipend, and support for suitable spaces |
| **SCs / STs** | Included in disadvantaged groups; coverage capped at 25% of urban poor population |
| **Women** | Included in disadvantaged groups; targeted for skill training and self-employment |
| **Minorities** | Included in disadvantaged groups; targeted for inclusion |
| **Differently-abled Persons** | Included in disadvantaged groups; eligible for training and support |
| **Self-Employment Programme (SEP) - Individual** | No minimum educational qualification required; training provided if special skills needed |
| **SEP - Group Enterprises** | Minimum 3 members; at least 70% from urban poor families; no more than one person from the same family |
| **Employment through Skills Training & Placement (EST&P)** | Eligibility varies by training type:<br>- Fresh entrants: Minimum 200 hours<br>- Reskilling: Minimum 16 hours<br>- Formal recognition: Bridge courses if needed |

### Benefits & Financial Support
Financial support under DAY-NULM includes multiple components tailored to different beneficiary groups and activities.

| Support Type | Details | Maximum Limit / Rate |
|--------------|---------|----------------------|
| **Interest Subsidy (Individual Micro-Enterprises)** | Covers difference between 7% p.a. and prevailing interest rate on bank loans | Project cost up to Rs. 2 lakhs |
| **Interest Subsidy (Group Enterprises)** | Covers difference between 7% p.a. and prevailing interest rate on bank loans | Project cost up to Rs. 10 lakhs |
| **Revolving Fund Support** | Provided to Self-Help Groups (SHGs) and their federations | As per SHG corpus and repayment capacity |
| **Seed Capital (PMFME Convergence)** | Rs. 40,000 per SHG member for working capital and small tools | Repayable as loan from SHG federations at interest ≤6% p.a. |
| **Credit-linked Subsidy (Food Processing)** | 35% of project cost | Up to Rs. 10 lakh |
| **City Livelihood Centres (CLCs) & Shelters** | Funding for construction and operation | Subject to availability and approval |
| **Capacity Building and Training (CB&T)** | Overall expenditure capped | 12% of total allocation |

**Non-financial benefits** include:
- Access to skill training for market-based jobs and self-employment
- Financial inclusion through basic savings accounts and credit linkage
- Identity cards and social security linkages for street vendors
- Training and stipend for street vendors
- Support for innovative and special projects
- Capacity building, institution formation, bank linkage, training of community organisers
- Convergence with other government schemes for entitlements like food, healthcare, education, and housing

### Application Process
The application process involves multiple stages from identification to subsidy release, coordinated between Urban Local Bodies (ULBs), Task Forces, banks, and beneficiaries.

```mermaid
flowchart TD
    A[Identify Prospective Beneficiaries] --> B[Submit Application of Intent to ULB]
    B --> C[ULB Enters Details into Register/MIS & Issues Acknowledgement]
    C --> D[ULB Screens Applications & Forwards Recommended Cases to Task Force]
    D --> E[Task Force Scrutinises Applications, Conducts Interviews, Recommends/Rejects]
    E --> F[Recommended Cases Sent to Concerned Banks for Processing within 15 Days]
    F --> G[Banks Process Loans & Submit Periodic Reports to ULB]
    G --> H[For Interest Subsidy: Banks Submit Quarterly Claims to ULB]
    H --> I[ULB Verifies & Releases Subsidy (Difference between 7% & Prevailing Rate) to Banks]
    E --> J[For SEP-G: Similar Process with Group Entry Form & Verification of Group Strength]
    E --> K[For SHG Bank Linkage: SHGs Apply through ULB; Subsidy Released on Timely Repayment]
    E --> L[For Innovative Projects: Proposals Submitted to State/UT Govt or SULM → Screened by National Mission Directorate → Approved by PAC]
    style A fill:#e3f2fd,stroke:#1565c0
    style I fill:#e8f5e9,stroke:#2e7d32
    style L fill:#fff3e0,stroke:#ef6c00
```

**Key Steps Explained:**
1. **Identification**: Through Community Organisers (COs) or ULB officials from urban poor households.
2. **Intent Submission**: Application with basic details (name, age, contact, address, Aadhaar, loan required, bank account, enterprise type) to ULB.
3. **Acknowledgement**: ULB enters details into MIS and issues unique registration number.
4. **Screening**: ULB screens and forwards recommended cases to ULB-level Task Force.
5. **Task Force Review**: Scrutinises viability, conducts interviews, recommends or rejects.
6. **Bank Processing**: Recommended cases sent to banks within 15 days; banks process loans.
7. **Reporting**: Banks submit periodic reports to ULB on application status.
8. **Interest Subsidy Claim**: Banks submit quarterly claims post-disbursement; ULB verifies and releases subsidy (difference between 7% and prevailing rate).
9. **Group Enterprises (SEP-G)**: Similar process with group entry form and verification of ≥70% urban poor membership and max 1 person/family.
10. **SHG Bank Linkage**: SHGs apply via ULB; subsidy released on timely repayment.
11. **Innovative Projects**: Proposals to State/UT Govt or SULM → screened by National Mission Directorate → approved by Project Approval Committee (PAC).

### Key Caveats
> **Critical Limitations and Conditions**
> - Interest subsidy is released **only on timely repayment** of loan.
> - For group enterprises under SEP: **minimum 3 members**, at least **70% from urban poor families**, and **no more than one person from the same family**.
> - Seed capital under PMFME convergence is **repayable as a loan** with interest **not exceeding 6% per annum**.
> - Funding for City Livelihood Centres and shelters is **subject to availability and approval**.
> - Training under EST&P requires **third-party certification and assessment**.
> - Shelter provision for urban homeless is to be achieved by **2022** as per guidelines.
> - Innovative & Special Projects (I&SP) component limited to **up to 5% of total central funds**.
