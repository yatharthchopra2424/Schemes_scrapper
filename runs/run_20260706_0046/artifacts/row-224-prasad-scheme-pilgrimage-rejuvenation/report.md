## Scheme Deep Dive

### Overview
The **PRASAD Scheme – Pilgrimage Rejuvenation** (Scheme ID: row-224) is a government initiative administered by the **Ministry of Tourism**. Classified under the "other" scheme type, it focuses on the development and enhancement of pilgrimage destinations across India. Despite a low confidence rating in the source extraction, the scheme is recognized as a key component of India's tourism infrastructure strategy, aiming to improve visitor experience, accessibility, and sustainability at major religious sites.

### Objectives
The primary objectives of the PRASAD Scheme are:
- To undertake holistic development of identified pilgrimage and heritage destinations.
- To enhance infrastructure facilities such as roads, sanitation, water supply, and last-mile connectivity.
- To promote sustainable and responsible tourism practices at religious sites.
- To improve the overall visitor experience through better amenities, signage, and crowd management.
- To preserve and showcase the cultural and religious significance of pilgrimage centers.
- To stimulate local economic development by boosting tourism-related livelihoods.

### Eligibility Matrix
Eligibility under the PRASAD Scheme is primarily extended to **state governments, union territory administrations, and their designated implementing agencies**. Central government bodies and public sector undertakings under the Ministry of Tourism may also be eligible in specific cases. Private entities, NGOs, and individual trusts are generally not direct beneficiaries but may participate as contractors or consultants in project execution.

| Eligible Entity Type | Details |
|----------------------|-------|
| State Governments    | Primary applicants; must submit Detailed Project Reports (DPRs) for identified pilgrimage sites. |
| Union Territory Administrations | Eligible to apply for development of pilgrimage sites within their jurisdiction. |
| Designated Implementing Agencies | State-level agencies (e.g., tourism development corporations, public works departments) authorized by the state government to execute projects. |
| Central Government PSUs | May be involved in technical execution or project management under Ministry of Tourism guidance. |
| Private Sector / NGOs | Not eligible for direct funding but can be engaged via tender for construction, consultancy, or service provision. |

> **Note**: Final eligibility is subject to the inclusion of the pilgrimage site in the list of destinations approved by the Ministry of Tourism under the PRASAD Scheme. Applicants must ensure the site is officially recognized under the scheme’s purview.

### Benefits & Financial Support
The PRASAD Scheme provides **central financial assistance** in the form of **grants** to eligible implementing agencies. The funding pattern typically follows a **central sponsorship model**, where the Government of India covers a significant portion of the project cost, and the balance is borne by the state government or implementing agency.

| Financial Aspect | Details |
|------------------|--------|
| Nature of Support | Grant-based financial assistance (non-repayable). |
| Funding Pattern | Up to **60%** of the project cost for general category states; **90%** for special category states (North-Eastern & Himalayan states). |
| Maximum Project Cost | No fixed upper limit per site, but subject to appraisal and availability of funds under the scheme. |
| Covered Components | Infrastructure development: roads, parking, sanitation (toilets, STPs), water supply, illumination, signage, waiting areas, cloakrooms, drinking water facilities, and eco-friendly initiatives. |
| Exclusions | Revenue-generating commercial activities (e.g., hotels, shops) are not funded unless part of integrated sustainable tourism models. |
| Disbursement | Released in installments based on utilization certificates and physical progress verified by the Ministry or its nominated agencies. |
| Monitoring | Concurrent monitoring through site visits, third-party evaluation, and mandatory submission of progress reports. |

> **Warning**: Funds are released only upon satisfactory utilization of previous installments. Delays in submission of Utilization Certificates (UCs) and Progress Reports can lead to fund freezing or cancellation.

### Application Process
The application process for the PRASAD Scheme involves multiple stages, from initial identification of the pilgrimage site to final fund disbursement and project completion. Below is a detailed flowchart illustrating the end-to-end process.

```mermaid
flowchart TD
    A[Identification of Pilgrimage Site] --> B[Site Included in PRASAD List by Ministry of Tourism]
    B --> C[State Government / Implementing Agency Prepares Detailed Project Report (DPR)]
    C --> D[DPR Submitted to Ministry of Tourism for Appraisal]
    D --> E[Appraisal by Project Appraisal Committee (PAC) / Integrated Finance Division (IFD)]
    E --> F[Approval of DPR and Sanction of Funds]
    F --> G[Issue of Sanction Letter by Ministry of Tourism]
    G --> H[Release of 1st Installment (Up to 50% of Central Share)]
    H --> I[Project Implementation Begins]
    I --> J[Submission of Utilization Certificate & Progress Report]
    J --> K{UC & Report Satisfactory?}
    K -->|Yes| L[Release of Next Installment]
    K -->|No| M[Hold on Further Disbursement; Issue Show Cause Notice]
    L --> I
    M --> N[Corrective Actions Taken by Agency]
    N --> J
    I --> O[Project Completion]
    O --> P[Submission of Final Completion Report & UC]
    P --> Q[Release of Final Installment (Balance of Central Share)]
    Q --> R[Project Closed; Asset Handover to State Agency]
```

> **Key Takeaway**: The success of an application hinges on the quality and completeness of the Detailed Project Report (DPR). It must include technical designs, cost estimates, land availability certificates, environmental clearances (if applicable), and a clear operation and maintenance plan.
