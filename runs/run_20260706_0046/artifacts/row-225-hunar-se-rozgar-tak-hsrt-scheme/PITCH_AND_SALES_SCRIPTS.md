# Pitch & Sales Scripts

## Philosophy
We are advisors, not salespeople. We diagnose first. We only pitch a scheme if it fits the client. We never oversell success rates.

---

## 1. Discovery Call Script (First Contact — 20 min)

### Opening (2 min)
Good morning/afternoon. I specialize in helping hospitality and tourism businesses access government skill development funding. The Hunar Se Rozgar Tak (HSRT) Scheme from the Ministry of Tourism provides stipends, certification, and placement support for youth and economically weaker sections seeking skills in hospitality trades like cooking, food service, and housekeeping. I’d like to understand your training needs to see if this scheme aligns with your goals.

### Discovery Questions
1. What is the primary hospitality trade you train or wish to train in (e.g., Multi Cuisine Cook, Food & Beverage Service, Room Attendant)?
2. What is the minimum educational qualification of your target trainees (e.g., 8th pass, 10th pass)?
3. Are your trainees primarily fresh entrants to the job market or already employed in hospitality seeking up-skilling?
4. What is the typical duration of your current training programmes (in hours)?
5. Do you have the infrastructure to run at least two HSRT-approved courses (e.g., kitchen lab, training restaurant)?
6. Are your trainees’ Aadhaar and bank accounts linked for stipend transfer via PFMS?
7. What is your annual training capacity (number of trainees you can accommodate)?
8. Are you currently empanelled with any government skill development scheme, or is this your first application?

### Qualification Checklist (internal — don't read aloud)
- [ ] Target trainees are above 18 years of age
- [ ] For fresh entrants: training duration minimum 200 hours (including practical/OJT)
- [ ] For re-skilling/up-skilling: training duration minimum 16 hours (including practical/OJT)
- [ ] Trainees possess minimum educational qualification as per course (e.g., 8th pass for Cook, 10th for F&B Steward)
- [ ] Applicant can run at least two NSQF-approved HSRT courses (Multi Cuisine Cook, F&B Service Steward, Room Attendant, Front Office Associate, etc.)
- [ ] Infrastructure available for classroom and practical training (as per Annexure-B requirements)
- [ ] Willing to maintain attendance records with minimum 80% for certification eligibility
- [ ] Prepared to upload video clippings on 10th and concluding day to pmuhsrt@gmail.com
- [ ] Willing to track trainees for one year post-certification for placement monitoring
- [ ] Not blacklisted by MSDE, NSDA, or Ministry of Tourism
- [ ] Ready to pay security deposit of Rs.20,000/- (if new applicant) or re-inspection fee of Rs.10,000/- if previously rejected

### Transition to Pitch
Based on your responses, the Hunar Se Rozgar Tak Scheme appears to be a strong fit—it offers up to Rs.11,340 per trainee in funding norms, includes a Rs.1,000 stipend, and provides certification and placement support. Let me walk you through how it works.

## 2. Scheme Pitch (5 min)

### Structure: Problem → Scheme → Value → Proof → Next Step

**Problem framing:**
Hospitality businesses struggle to find skilled workers, while youth from economically weaker sections lack access to certified, job-ready training. Many informal skills go unrecognised, and placement support is weak, leading to high turnover and underemployment in the sector.

**Scheme intro:**
The Hunar Se Rozgar Tak (HSRT) Scheme, administered by the Ministry of Tourism, Government of India, is a subsidy-based skill development programme offering financial norms of Rs.11,340 per trainee for hospitality trades. This includes training fee (Rs.5,000), course material (Rs.500), certification (Rs.1,000), apron (Rs.300), lunch (Rs.3,000), stipend (Rs.1,000), and administrative expenses (5%, Rs.540). Funds are released in instalments based on outcomes achieved.

**Why they qualify (personalised):**
- Training duration of minimum 200 hours for fresh entrants (or 16 hours for up-skilling)
- Target beneficiaries include youth, economically weaker sections, unemployed persons, and those seeking hospitality skills
- Must maintain minimum 80% attendance for stipend and certification eligibility
- Willing to undergo third-party assessment with one internal and one external examiner

Approval is tied to verifiable results, not just enrollment—we’ve helped providers meet these milestones. |

| "I'll try it myself first" | Common mistakes include: using the same entity as trainer and assessor (conflict of interest), and failing to maintain 80% attendance—both lead to rejection or fund withholding. We ensure compliance from day one. |
| "We're too small / too big" | The scheme targets training providers with capacity for at least 20–30 trainees per batch. There’s no upper limit—funding scales with outcomes. Even small private institutes can apply if they have infrastructure for two HSRT courses. |
| "I don't have the documents ready" | The three most commonly missing documents are: 1) Proof of Aadhar-linked bank account for stipend transfer, 2) Course-specific infrastructure proof (e.g., kitchen lab, training restaurant), and 3) Utilization certificates and monthly progress reports from past batches. |
