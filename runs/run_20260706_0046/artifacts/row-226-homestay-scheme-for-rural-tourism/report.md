# Comprehensive Scheme Masterclass & File Guide

## Scheme Deep Dive

### Scheme Overview
The **Homestay Scheme for Rural Tourism** (Scheme ID: row-226) is a **grant-type** scheme launched by the **Ministry of Tourism**, Government of India, aimed at promoting responsible tourism in tribal areas across **30 States/UTs**. The scheme focuses on developing homestays in tribal villages to enhance livelihood opportunities, preserve cultural heritage, and create authentic rural tourism experiences. The implementing agency is designated by the **State Government / UT Administrations**, and applications are submitted via the portal **sd2.tourism.gov.in**. The scheme was last updated in **2025** and remains active with timelines communicated periodically by the Ministry of Tourism.

### Objectives
- Develop homestays in tribal areas to promote responsible tourism  
- Enhance livelihood opportunities for tribal communities  
- Create community-based responsible tourism through cultural exchange  
- Provide authentic tribal rural experience for tourists  
- Encourage states/UTs to provide further assistance to uplift local livelihood  
- Develop tourism potential in tribal villages  
- Promote entrepreneurship and self-employment in the tourism value chain  
- Ensure convergence with existing Government of India/State/UT schemes

> **> Important Notes on Application Process:**  
> - States/UTs must submit **one project proposal** under this scheme, which may include a cluster of 5-6 villages with 5-10 homestays per village.  
> - The **covering letter** from the State/UT Tourism Secretary is mandatory for portal submission.  
> - In case of technical issues with the portal **sd2.tourism.gov.in**, proposals may be submitted via email to **swadesh.darshan@gov.in**, with simultaneous notification to the Ministry.  
> - All proposals are evaluated by the **Mission Directorate (MD)** and then forwarded to the **Central Sanctioning and Monitoring Committee (CSMC)** for final approval.  
> - The **decision of MoT on funding amount is final**; higher allocations (up to Rs. 2 Cr) are considered only after exhaustion of the Rs. 5 Cr ceiling and recommendation by MD and CSMC.  
> - **States/UTs must ensure convergence** with central and state schemes during village and homestay identification.  
> - **Technical upskilling** of homestay owners must be conducted in collaboration with **IHMs, SIHMs, and Ministry of Tribal Affairs**.  
> - **State/UT is responsible** for capacity building, marketing, promotion, and post-development monitoring (revenue, occupancy, feedback).  

### Key Caveats & Warnings
> **> Critical Restrictions:**  
> - Funding **cannot** be utilized for land acquisition, resettlement, rehabilitation, relocation, or dredging/development of bunds of water bodies (man-made or natural).  
> - State/UT **must ensure** the proposed land parcel is in the beneficiary’s possession with clear ownership title and available for development.  
> - State/UT **must ensure** the identified homestay owner belongs to the tribal community.  
> - State/UT **must provide** requisite capacity building support (upskilling/reskilling) to beneficiaries during development and be responsible for marketing/promotion post-development.  
> - State/UT **should partner** with experienced NGO/Rural Tourism Societies for identification, stakeholder consultations, sensitization, community development, and implementation.  
> - State/UT **may assist** in upgrading hospitality experience using state/UT funds and encourage sustainable tourism practices.  
> - **Technical upskilling** of homestay owners must be done by States/UTs in collaboration with IHMs, SIHMs, and Ministry of Tribal Affairs.
