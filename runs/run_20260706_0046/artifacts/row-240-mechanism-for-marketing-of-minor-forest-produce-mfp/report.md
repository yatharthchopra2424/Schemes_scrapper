# Comprehensive Scheme Masterclass & File Guide

## Scheme Deep Dive

### Scheme Overview
The **Mechanism for Marketing of Minor Forest Produce (MFP)** is a central sector subsidy scheme launched by the Ministry of Tribal Affairs, Government of India, with TRIFED (Tribal Cooperative Marketing Development Federation of India Limited) as the implementing agency. The scheme operates pan-India and aims to institutionalize the entire value chain of MFP—from sustainable collection to value addition, processing, infrastructure development, and marketing—to ensure fair and remunerative prices for tribal MFP gatherers.

**Scheme ID:** row-240  
**Ministry / Category:** Tribal Welfare  
**Scheme Type:** Subsidy  
**Geographic Scope:** Pan-India (all states and union territories)  
**Implementing Agency:** Tribal Cooperative Marketing Development Federation of India Limited (TRIFED)  
**Application Portal:** https://trifed.tribal.gov.in  
**Key Sources:**  
- Guidelines for Mechanism for Marketing of Minor Forest Produce (MFP) Through Minimum Support Price (MSP) & Development of Value Chain (DVC) for MFP (Notification F.No. 19/17/2018 – Livelihood dated 26.02.2019)  
- TRIFED official website and program pages  
- Annual reports and advisory documents

### Objectives
The scheme’s objectives are explicitly defined in the evidence and include:

- Provide Minimum Support Price (MSP) to tribal MFP gatherers for forest produces collected by them  
- Bridge the last mile connectivity between tribals and markets through activation of Haat bazars by strengthening grassroots infrastructure for MFP procurement, storage, transportation, marketing  
- Provide training and skill upgradation of tribal artisans in a systematic manner by introducing value addition and marketing through tribal groups and clusters  
- Promote sustainable livelihoods opportunities in tribal areas by creating sustainable value chains of MFP  
- Strengthen linkage to women’s financial empowerment as MFP collection and value addition activity is undertaken by women MFP gatherers  
- Ensure fair deal to tribals by offering MSP and provisions for value addition  
- Enhance tribal income and improve quality of life through sustainable and comprehensive implementation of the scheme  
- Achieve convergence and coherence with other development partners working in the field of tribal development  

> **Key Takeaway:** The scheme is not merely a price support mechanism but a holistic livelihood intervention designed to transform tribal economies by moving beyond raw material collection to value addition and market linkage.

### Eligibility Matrix
Eligibility is broad and inclusive, targeting specific beneficiary groups and covering a defined set of MFPs across all Indian states and union territories.

| **Eligibility Criteria** | **Details** | **Source** |
|--------------------------|-----------|------------|
| **Covered MFPs** | 50 non-nationalized Minor Forest Produces (Annexure 1 of the guidelines) | Guidelines, Section 1 |
| **Geographic Coverage** | All states and union territories of India | Guidelines, Section 1; Key Facts |
| **Implementing Structure** | Designed for implementation through State and District level functionaries | Guidelines, Section 1; Key Facts |
| **Target Beneficiaries** | Tribal MFP gatherers; Self Help Groups (SHGs); Van Dhan Vikas Kendras (VDVKs); tribal artisans; forest dwellers | Key Facts |
| **Nodal Ministry** | Ministry of Tribal Affairs (MoTA) – Central Apex Committee responsible for policy, oversight, and budgetary resources | Guidelines, Section 2(i); Key Facts |
| **Technical Support Unit** | TRIFED – coordinates implementation and flow of funds through State and District Implementing Units via an Executive Committee | Guidelines, Section 2(ii); Key Facts |
| **MSP Recommendation** | Pricing Cell setup in TRIFED recommends MSP; announcement done by MoTA | Guidelines, Section 2(iii); Key Facts |
| **State Role** | State Government identifies State Nodal Department and District Implementing Units; State Nodal Department coordinates fund flow | Guidelines, Section 2(iv); Key Facts |

> **Note:** The scheme explicitly includes all states and UTs, with no income or turnover limits for beneficiaries. Eligibility is based on identity as a tribal MFP gatherer or associated entity (SHG, VDVK, artisan) and engagement with covered MFPs.

### Benefits & Financial Support
The scheme delivers multi-dimensional benefits through institutionalized value chain interventions. Financial support flows via a structured mechanism involving central and state contributions.

#### Financial Support Mechanism
| **Component** | **Role** | **Details** | **Source** |
|---------------|---------|-------------|------------|
| **Ministry of Tribal Affairs (MoTA)** | Nodal Ministry / Central Apex Committee | Responsible for policy, oversight, obtaining and providing budgetary resources for the scheme | Guidelines, Section 2(i); Key Facts |
| **TRIFED** | Technical Support Unit | Coordinates implementation and flow of funds through State and District Implementing Units via an Executive Committee | Guidelines, Section 2(ii); Key Facts |
| **Pricing Cell (TRIFED)** | MSP Recommendation | Recommends Minimum Support Price for MFPs | Guidelines, Section 2(iii); Key Facts |
| **MoTA** | MSP Announcement | Officially announces the MSP for MFPs | Guidelines, Section 2(iii); Key Facts |
| **State Government** | Implementation Oversight | Identifies State Nodal Department and District Implementing Units | Guidelines, Section 2(iv); Key Facts |
| **State Nodal Department** | Fund Flow Coordination | Coordinates flow of funds and related oversight to District Units | Guidelines, Section 2(iv); Key Facts |
| **Fund Sharing Ratio** | Central:State | 75:25 for procurement, infrastructure, training, value addition, and marketing (Van Dhan) | Guidelines, Sections 2(vii), 2(viii), 3, 7(iii)(f), 7(ix)(f) |
| **Overhead Expenses** | Implementation Support | Up to 20% of budgeted procurement outlay for Programme Management Units (PMUs) at state/district levels | Guidelines, Sections 2(vii)(a), 2(viii)(e), 2(ix)(k), 2(x)(p) |
| **Procurement Agency Commission** | SHGs/LAMPS/etc. | Commission not exceeding 7.5% of MFP value procured through them | Guidelines, Section 2(vii)(a) |
| **District Incentive** | District Implementing Unit | Incentive not exceeding 2% of base procurement value of MFP | Guidelines, Section 7(ix)(b) |
| **Audit Cost Sharing** | Central:State | Shared in ratio of 75:25, up to 33% of basic procurement price | Guidelines, Section 7(iii)(n) |

### Application Process
The application process is decentralized and implemented through State and District level functionaries. There is no centralized online portal for individual beneficiary applications; instead, implementation occurs via government-led procurement and value addition units at Haat bazars and VDVKs.

> **Process Notes:**  
> - There is no direct "application" by individuals; benefits are accessed through participation in state-led procurement at Haat bazars, membership in SHGs/VDVKs, and engagement with TRIFED-organized value addition and marketing initiatives.  
> - Tribals access MSP by selling their produce to authorized procurement agencies (SHGs, LAMPS, etc.) at Haat bazars, which are then paid by District Implementing Units.  
> - VDVKs are set up as hubs for training, value addition, and marketing, funded via the scheme.  
> - All financial flows are monitored via an IT-enabled MIS platform set up by TRIFED.  
> - The process is continuous and cyclical, tied to seasonal MFP availability and procurement planning.
