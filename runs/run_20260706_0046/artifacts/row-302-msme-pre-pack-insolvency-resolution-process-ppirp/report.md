# Comprehensive Scheme Masterclass & File Guide

## Scheme Deep Dive

### Scheme Overview
The **MSME Pre-Pack Insolvency Resolution Process (PPIRP)** is a specialized insolvency resolution mechanism designed exclusively for Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs) under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (IBC). Administered by the **Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India (IBBI)**, PPIRP enables faster, cost-effective resolution by allowing the corporate debtor to initiate the process with creditor consent, avoiding the public invitation and e-auction requirements of the regular Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP). The scheme aims to preserve business continuity, maximize stakeholder value, and reduce procedural delays and costs.

**Application Portal**: https://ibbi.gov.in/intimation-applications/apply-iaaa  
**Implementing Agency**: Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India  
**Geographic Scope**: Pan-India  
**Last Updated**: 2026  
**Scheme ID**: row-302  
**Ministry/Category**: Legal & Regulatory  
**Scheme Type**: Other  
**Target Beneficiaries**: MSME  

### Objectives
- Facilitate faster resolution of insolvency for MSMEs  
- Reduce the cost and time associated with the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP)  
- Enable the corporate debtor to initiate resolution with creditor consent  
- Preserve business continuity and maximize value for stakeholders  
- Provide a mechanism for early identification and exclusion of non-viable projects  
- Strengthen project-wise ring-fencing of funds and accounts in real estate insolvency (as part of broader IBC framework)  
- Ensure timely handover of possession to allottees in real estate projects  
- Enhance transparency and accountability of insolvency professionals  

### Eligibility Matrix
| Criteria | Requirement | Source |
|---------|-------------|--------|
| Corporate Debtor Classification | Must be classified as a Micro, Small, or Medium Enterprise as per the Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises Development Act, 2006 | Key Facts |
| Financial Creditor Consent | Requires consent of not less than sixty-six percent (66%) of financial creditors by value | Key Facts |
| No Pending Insolvency Application | No pending application for insolvency resolution under section 7, 9, or 10 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 | Key Facts |
| No Liquidation Order | No order for liquidation has been passed against the corporate debtor | Key Facts |
| No Recent PPIRP | The corporate debtor must not have undergone a PPIRP in the last three years | Key Facts |
| Resolution Applicant Eligibility | The successful resolution applicant must be eligible under section 29A of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 | Key Facts |
| Resolution Plan Compliance | The resolution plan must comply with section 30(2) of the Code, including payment of CIRP costs and operational creditor dues in priority | Key Facts |
| Exclusions | Does not apply to personal guarantors to corporate debtors | Key Facts |

> **Warning**: PPIRP is not available if a corporate insolvency resolution process has already been initiated under section 7, 9, or 10 of the Code, or if an order for liquidation has been passed. A corporate debtor cannot undergo PPIRP more than once in a period of three years.

### Benefits & Financial Support
PPIRP does **not** involve direct financial support from the government. Instead, it facilitates resolution through private infusion of funds by the resolution applicant, which may include fresh equity, quasi-equity, or internal accruals. The financial support is structured as part of the resolution plan.

| Aspect | Details | Source |
|--------|---------|--------|
| Financial Support Mechanism | Private infusion of funds by the resolution applicant (fresh equity, quasi-equity, or internal accruals) as part of the resolution plan | Key Facts |
| Quantum of Support | Varies case-by-case based on the resolution plan approved by the Committee of Creditors (CoC) and the Adjudicating Authority | Key Facts |
| Performance Guarantee | Typically 10% of the resolution plan value, as per regulation 36B(4A) of CIRP Regulations | Key Facts |
| Primary Benefit | Faster resolution timeline (typically within 90 days) compared to regular CIRP (180 days, extendable to 330 days) | Key Facts |
| Cost Reduction | Avoids public invitation of resolution plans and e-auctions, reducing procedural costs | Key Facts |
| Management Continuity | Allows existing management to retain control if the resolution plan is approved, promoting business continuity | Key Facts |
| Structural Flexibility | Permits merger, amalgamation, or takeover in the resolution plan | Key Facts |
| Creditor Benefit | Higher realization due to reduced delays and preservation of going concern value | Key Facts |
| Operational Continuity | Minimizes disruption to ongoing operations and preserves employment | Key Facts |

### Required Documents
1. Resolution Plan  
2. Information Memorandum  
3. Evaluation Matrix  
4. Declaration under Section 29A of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016  
5. Proof of consent of financial creditors (not less than 66% by value)  
6. Certificate of incorporation of the corporate debtor  
7. Latest audited financial statements  
8. Details of claims of financial and operational creditors  
9. Valuation reports (if any)  
10. Performance guarantee (as per regulation 36B(4A) of CIRP Regulations)  

### Application Process
The PPIRP application process involves the following steps, initiated by the corporate debtor in consultation with its financial creditors:

```mermaid
flowchart TD
    A[Corporate debtor prepares resolution plan in consultation with financial creditors] --> B[Resolution plan approved by Committee of Creditors (CoC) with ≥66% vote of financial creditors by value]
    B --> C[Corporate debtor files application for initiation of PPIRP before Adjudicating Authority (NCLT) under section 30(6) of IBC, 2016]
    C --> D[Application accompanied by resolution plan, information memorandum, evaluation matrix, and declaration under section 29A of the Code]
    D --> E[Adjudicating Authority examines application for compliance with section 30(2) and 29A of the Code]
    E --> F{Is application compliant?}
    F -->|Yes| G[Adjudicating Authority approves resolution plan, making it binding on all stakeholders]
    F -->|No| H[Application rejected if resolution plan is not feasible or viable]
    G --> I[Successful resolution applicant implements plan, including infusion of funds and payment of performance guarantee]
    I --> J[Resolution process must be completed within 90 days from date of application]
    J --> K{Exceptional circumstances?}
    K -->|Yes| L[Adjudicating Authority may extend process by further 60 days (max 150 days total)]
    K -->|No| M[Process concludes at 90 days]
```

**Key Timelines**:  
- No fixed application window; can be initiated at any time subject to eligibility  
- Resolution process must be completed within **90 days** from date of application  
- Extendable by Adjudicating Authority for a further **60 days** in exceptional circumstances (maximum **150 days**)  

### Critical Process Notes
- The resolution plan must be approved by the CoC with not less than 66% of financial creditors by value  
- The Adjudicating Authority (NCLT) scrutinizes the plan for compliance with sections 30(2) and 29A of the IBC  
- Upon approval, the resolution plan becomes binding on all stakeholders (financial creditors, operational creditors, employees, government authorities, etc.)  
- The successful resolution applicant must infuse funds to settle claims, pay CIRP costs, and provide performance guarantee  
- PPIRP avoids the public invitation and e-auction stages of CIRP, reducing time and cost  
- The process preserves going concern value and minimizes disruption to operations  

> **Takeaway**: PPIRP is a creditor-consent-driven, time-bound mechanism tailored for MSMEs, leveraging private capital to achieve faster, cheaper insolvency resolution while preserving business value—distinct from the longer, costlier public CIRP process.
