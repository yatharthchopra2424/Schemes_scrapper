# Comprehensive Scheme Masterclass & File Guide

## Scheme Deep Dive

### Overview
The **National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT)** is a quasi-judicial body constituted under Section 408 of the Companies Act, 2013, effective from 1st June 2016. It operates under the Ministry of Corporate Affairs, Government of India, and serves as a unified forum for adjudicating disputes under the Companies Act, 2013, and the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC), 2016. The NCLT does not provide direct financial support such as grants, loans, or subsidies. Instead, financial outcomes arise from court-ordered resolutions, including debt restructuring, liquidation proceeds, or repayment plans under IBC.

### Objectives
- To provide a unified forum for adjudication of disputes under the Companies Act and Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code.
- To promote corporate governance and legal compliance while safeguarding stakeholder interests.
- To facilitate revival and rehabilitation of financially distressed companies through timely insolvency resolution.
- To maximize asset value and promote entrepreneurship and availability of credit.
- To balance stakeholder interests, including alteration in government dues payment priority.
- To establish the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India and handle connected matters.
- To reduce NPAs substantially as an ancillary benefit of IBC implementation.
- To resolve insolvency of individual debtors (personal guarantors) and restore their financial viability.

### Eligibility Matrix
The NCLT has jurisdiction over:
- Company law cases transferred from Hon’ble High Courts and the erstwhile Company Law Tribunal.
- Insolvency cases under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016.
- Matters relating to oppression and mismanagement, class action suits, reduction of share capital, rectification of register of members, amalgamations, mergers, restoration of company name, winding up, and other functions under the Companies Act.
- Exclusive jurisdiction to commence and adjudicate Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP) cases and Insolvency Resolution Process for Personal Guarantors to Corporate Debtors.

**Target Beneficiaries**: MSMEs, corporates, creditors, shareholders, employees, personal guarantors.

| **Eligibility Criteria** | **Details** |
|--------------------------|-----------|
| **Jurisdiction** | Bench-specific; cases must be filed before the bench having territorial and subject-matter jurisdiction. |
| **Case Types** | Company Petitions, Company Appeals, Insolvency Petitions under Companies Act, 2013 or IBC, 2016. |
| **Procedural Requirements** | Proper indexing, OCR compliance, signed pages, correct case type, and complete documentation to avoid objections. |
| **Timelines** | Compliance with timelines prescribed under IBC for insolvency proceedings (e.g., 180-day CIRP timeline, extendable by 90 days). |
| **Geographic Scope** | Pan-India, with 16 benches located across states and union territories. |

### Application Process
The application process is judicial and procedural, involving filing petitions, document submission, hearings, and adjudication. Below is a Mermaid.js flowchart illustrating the steps:

```mermaid

    A[Identify Appropriate NCLT Bench] --> B[File Relevant Petition/Application]

    E --> F[Attend Hearings as Scheduled]
    F --> G[Await Judgment/Order from NCLT Bench]
    G --> H[Implementation of Court-Ordered Resolution]
    H --> I[Outcomes: Debt Restructuring, Liquidation, Repayment Plan, etc.]

    subgraph Required Documents
        direction TB
        D1[Certificate of Incorporation/Registration]
        D2[Memorandum and Articles of Association]

        D4[Statement of Accounts and Audit Reports]
        D5[Details of Creditors and Dues Owed]
        D6[Evidence of Default (for IBC cases)]
        D7[Proposed Resolution Plan (for CIRP)]
        D8[Affidavit Verifying Petition Contents]
        D9[Vakalatnama (if represented by counsel)]
        D10[Proof of Payment of Court Fees]
        D11[Any Other Document as Directed by Tribunal]
    end
```

**Key Procedural Steps**:
1. Identify the appropriate NCLT bench based on geographical jurisdiction (refer to Jurisdiction of NCLT Benches table below).
2. File the relevant petition or application (e.g., Company Petition, Insolvency Petition) under the applicable provision of Companies Act, 2013 or Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016.
3. Submit the application along with required documents at the NCLT registry or through e-filing portal.
4. Pay the prescribed court fees.
5. Serve notice to the opposite party as per rules.
6. Attend hearings as scheduled by the Tribunal.
7. Await judgment or order from the NCLT bench.

### Key Caveats
> **Jurisdiction is bench-specific**; cases must be filed before the bench having territorial and subject-matter jurisdiction.  
> **Procedural defects** (e.g., incorrect case type, missing documents, improper indexing, lack of OCR, unsigned pages) may lead to objections and rejection.  
> During office relocation, helpdesk numbers may be temporarily non-functional.  
> **NCLT does not provide financial assistance**; outcomes depend on judicial orders.  
> Parties must comply with timelines prescribed under IBC for insolvency proceedings (e.g., CIRP must be completed within 180 days, extendable by 90 days).  
> The NCLT e-filing portal is accessible at https://nclt.gov.in for filing applications and tracking case status.
