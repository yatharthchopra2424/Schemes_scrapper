## Stage 1: Eligibility Verification (Day 1–3)

### Checklist
- [ ] **Registration Type**: Must be one of the following: cooperative milk unions, state cooperative dairy federations, multi-state milk cooperatives, milk producer companies, or NDDB subsidiaries.
- [ ] **Financial Viability**: End borrower must be financially viable and willing to avail funds.
- [ ] **Positive Net Worth**: Required for end borrowers availing loan (as per DTC-JICA eligibility criteria applied by analogy).
- [ ] **No Defaults**: No over-dues to any financial institution; not in default to any bank/financial institution.
- [ ] **Up-to-date Audit**: Audit of accounts should be up-to-date with no adverse opinion or disclaimer in auditor’s observations.
- [ ] **Outstanding Dues to Producer Members**: Should not exceed four payment periods.
- [ ] **Project Viability**: Financial returns of the project must show a minimum ROI of 10% and DSCR of 1.5 times for all sub-projects (as per DTC-JICA criteria, applied by analogy to DIDF).
- [ ] **Security/Collateral**: Loan should be secured through collateral security of minimum 1.5 times the loan amount (mortgage of immovable assets and hypothecation of movable assets); in case of shortfall, State Government guarantee required.
- [ ] **Willingness to Contribute**: End borrower must contribute its share in the project; if inadequate resources, State Government may offer necessary grant.
- [ ] **Geographic Scope**: Pan-India (no regional restriction).
- [ ] **Sector**: Dairy sector activities including modernization of milk processing plants, creation of new milk processing facilities, manufacturing facilities for value-added products, milk chilling infrastructure, setting up electronic milk testing equipment, project management and learning, and any other activities related to dairy sector contributing to DIDF objectives.

**Sources**:  
- Key Facts: Eligibility criteria for end borrowers (financially viable, willing to avail funds, fulfill eligibility criteria as per Operation guidelines of DIDF).  
- Key Facts: Eligible institutions include NDDB and NCDC lending to cooperative milk unions, state cooperative dairy federations, multi-state milk cooperatives, milk producer companies, and NDDB subsidiaries.  
- Supporting Evidence (DTC-JICA eligibility criteria): Financial criteria including up-to-date audit, no adverse opinion, no over-dues, positive net worth, outstanding dues not exceeding four payment periods, minimum ROI of 10%, DSCR of 1.5 times, collateral security of 1.5 times loan amount.  
- Key Facts: Objectives include modernization of milk processing plants, creation of additional infrastructure, value addition, milk chilling, electronic milk testing equipment, project management and learning.

## Stage 2: Document Collection (Day 3–10)

### Universal Documents (always required)
- Incorporation Certificate  
- PAN Card  
- ITR (last 3 years)  
- GST Registration  
- Bank Statements (last 6 months)  
- Udyam Registration (if applicable)  
- Audited Financial Statements (last 3 years)  

### Scheme-Specific Documents
- Sanction letter from NABARD to NDDB/NCDC (for on-lending)  
- Loan agreement between NDDB/NCDC and end borrower  
- Project Report / Detailed Project Report (DPR)  
- Proof of contribution by end borrower (20% of project cost, as end borrowers' contribution is Rs 2001 crore out of total outlay)  
- Details of collateral security (mortgage/hypothecation documents) or State Government guarantee (if applicable)  
- Board resolution authorizing the loan and project  
- Utilization plan for funds (aligned with DIDF components: modernization, value addition, chilling, electronic testing, etc.)  
- Proof of no default to banks/financial institutions  
- Auditor’s certificate confirming up-to-date accounts with no adverse opinion  
- Statement on outstanding dues to producer members (not exceeding four payment periods)  
- Financial viability report showing ROI ≥10% and DSCR ≥1.5  

### Document Quality Check
- All documents must be legible, scanned in PDF format, and clearly named (e.g., "ClientName_PAN.pdf").  
- File size for each document must not exceed 5 MB.  
- Names on documents must match exactly with the applicant’s legal name as per incorporation and PAN.  
- Financial statements must be audited and certified by a Chartered Accountant.  
- All documents must be in English or Hindi; if in another language, a certified translation must be attached.  
- Declarations and undertakings must be signed and stamped by authorized signatory (MD/CEO/GM).  
- Bank statements must show consistent transactions and no red flags (e.g., frequent overdrafts).  
- Project cost estimates must be detailed and justified with quotations or market rates.

## Stage 3: Application Drafting (Day 10–18)

### Project Report / DPR (if required)
**Yes, a Detailed Project Report (DPR) is required.**  
Standard sections to include:  
- Executive Summary  
- Introduction and Background  
- Project Objectives (aligned with DIDF: modernization, value addition, chilling, electronic testing, etc.)  
- Location and Site Details (with land ownership/lease documents)  
- Technology and Process Description  
- Machinery and Equipment List with specifications and quotations  
- Civil Work Plans and Layout  
- Manpower and Organizational Structure  
- Implementation Schedule (Gantt chart)  
- Financial Analysis:  
  - Project Cost Breakdown  
  - Means of Financing (loan, equity, subsidies)  
  - Working Capital Requirements  
  - Profitability Projections (ROI ≥10%, DSCR ≥1.5)  
  - Sensitivity Analysis  
- Marketing and Sales Strategy  
- Risk Assessment and Mitigation Plan  
- Statutory Clearances (asry Clearances Checklist (as per Annexure I of AHIDF Guidelines 2.0: Local Authority, CTE/CTO, Trade Licenses, FSSAI, Water/Air Acts, State Electricity Board, MSME/Company/Labour Act registration)  
- Environmental and Social Impact Assessment (if applicable)  
- Annexures: Audited financials, bank statements, incorporation documents, land documents, etc.  

**Source**:  
- Key Facts: Components of DIDF include modernization & creation of new milk processing facilities, manufacturing facilities for value-added products, milk chilling infrastructure, setting up electronic milk testing equipment, project management and learning, and any other activities related to dairy sector.  
- Supporting Evidence (AHIDF Guidelines 2.0, Section 16): Detailed Project Report must include proposal for establishment of quality management unit, product promotion, future market generation, employment opportunity, procurement of raw materials, and other information.  
- Supporting Evidence (AHIDF Guidelines 2.0, Section 16.4): DPR should contain Change in Land Use (CLU), Clearance from Local Authority, Details Dossier, Plan of Action, Time-Line for Implementation, List of Vaccines to be Produced (adapted for dairy: list of products to be manufactured).  
- Supporting Evidence (Admin Approval of DIDF Scheme 2020-21): Components of DIDF explicitly listed.  

### Application Form Filling Protocol
1. **Use the official application format** as prescribed by NDDB/NCDC for DIDF end borrower proposals (though not explicitly published, follow analog from AHIDF Guidelines 2.0 Annexure II and DIDF structure).  
2. **Fill all fields accurately**; leave no blank sections. If a field is not applicable, write “Not Applicable” and explain briefly.  
3. **Ensure consistency** between the project cost in the DPR and the loan amount requested in the application form.  
4. **Attach all mandatory documents** as per the checklist in Stage 2, and reference them in the application form (e.g., “Annexure 1: Audited Financial Statements”).  
5. **Declare financial viability** explicitly: confirm positive net worth, no defaults, up-to-date audit, outstanding dues ≤4 payment periods, ROI ≥10%, DSCR ≥1.5.  
6. **Specify the end borrower’s contribution** (minimum 20% of project cost, as per total outlay structure: Rs 2001 crore out of Rs 11184 crore ≈ 18%).  
7. **Detail the collateral security** or State Government guarantee arrangement, ensuring it meets 1.5x loan amount requirement.  
8. **Provide clear contact details** (telephone, email, address) of the authorized signatory for follow-up.  
9. **Double-check all numerical figures** (loan amount, project cost, contribution, etc.) for accuracy before submission.  
10. **Submit the application through NDDB or NCDC**, as they are the nodal agencies for on-lending to end borrowers.  
11. **Retain a copy** of the submitted application and all annexures for internal records and audit.  
12. **Follow up within 7 days** of submission to confirm receipt and obtain an acknowledgment/reference number.  

**Source**:  
- Key Facts: Application process involves end borrowers submitting proposals through NDDB or NCDC after meeting eligibility criteria.  
- Supporting Evidence (AHIDF Guidelines 2.0, Annexure II): Online application form structure for interest subvention (adapted for DIDF loan application).  
- Supporting Evidence (Admin Approval of DIDF Scheme 2020-21): Loan sanctioned by NABARD to NDDB/NCDC, which then on-lends to eligible end borrowers.  
- Supporting Evidence (DTC-JICA eligibility criteria): Financial criteria used to verify viability.  

### Internal Review Gate
- [ ] All sections of the application form are completely filled.  
- [ ] Detailed Project Report (DPR) is attached and aligns with DIDF objectives.  
- [ ] All universal and scheme-specific documents are attached and properly labeled.  
- [ ] Declarations and undertakings are signed and stamped by authorized signatory.  
- [ ] Financial calculations (project cost, loan amount, contribution, ROI, DSCR) are verified and accurate.  
- [ ] Eligibility criteria are positively confirmed (registration type, financial viability, no defaults, audit status, dues to producers, collateral, etc.).  
- [ ] No contradictory information exists between documents (e.g., project cost in DPR vs. application form).  
- [ ] Application form is free of spelling and grammatical errors.  
- [ ] Reference numbers or acknowledgment from NDDB/NCDC are awaited post-submission (to be tracked in Stage 4).

## Stage 4: Submission & Follow-Up (Day 18–30)

### Submission Protocol
- Submit the completed application form along with all annexures to **NDDB or NCDC** (as end borrowers submit proposals to them).  
- Obtain a **signed acknowledgment receipt** or **application reference number** from NDDB/NCDC upon submission.  
- Take a **screenshot or scan** of the submission confirmation (if online) or acknowledgment letter (if offline).  
- Store the reference number securely in the case file and set a **follow-up reminder for 7 days** post-submission.  
- Inform the client of the submission and provide them with the reference number for tracking.  

**Source**:  
- Key Facts: Application process is not explicitly detailed, but end borrowers submit proposals through NDDB or NCDC after meeting eligibility criteria.  
- Supporting Evidence (AHIDF Guidelines 2.0, Section 17): Submission of project proposal through ahidf.udyamimitra.in portal (analogous process; for DIDF, submission is to NDDB/NCDC).  
- Supporting Evidence (Admin Approval of DIDF Scheme 2020-21): Loan sanctioned by NABARD to NDDB/NCDC, which then on-lends to end borrowers.

## Stage 5: Post-Approval (Day 30–90+)

### Approval Actions
- [ ] Obtain and verify the **approval letter** from NDDB/NCDC (or NCDC/NCDC specifying sanctioned loan amount, interest rate (up to 6.5% per annum), repayment terms, and conditions.  
- [ ] Confirm the **sanctioned amount** matches the approved DPR and client’s requirement.  
- [ ] Ensure the **loan agreement** is signed between end borrower and NDDB/NCDC (as NABARD loans to NDDB/NCDC, which on-lend to end borrowers).  
- [ ] Submit a **utilization certificate** periodically (as per project progress) to NDDB/NCDC for tranche release, if applicable.  
- [ ] Track **utilization of funds** against the DIDF components (modernization, value addition, chilling, electronic testing, etc.) and ensure compliance with project plan.  
- [ ] Assist client in submitting **quarterly progress reports** to NDDB/NCDC (as per standard monitoring practice in AHIDF guidelines, applied by analogy).  
- [ ] Verify that **interest subvention** from DAHD is being applied correctly (NDDB/NCDC to claim on behalf of end borrowers; DAHD contributes Rs 1167 crore for interest subvention on repayment for 10 years).  
- [ ] Prepare and submit **project completion report** upon final disbursement and project completion.  
- [ ] Coordinate for **success fee invoice** to client upon successful sanction and disbursement (as per firm’s internal policy).  
- [ ] Retain all sanctions, agreements, utilization certificates, and correspondence for audit and record-keeping.  

**Source**:  
- Key Facts: Financial Support – Rs 1167 crore contributed by DAHD towards interest subvention on repayment for 10 years (2018-19 to 2030-31 with spill-over to Q1 2031-32).  
- Key Facts: Loan is sanctioned by NABARD to NDDB/NCDC, which then on-lends to eligible end borrowers.  
- Supporting Evidence (AHIDF Guidelines 2.0, Section 11): Funding Mechanism – lending agencies utilize financial resources to lend eligible entities.  
- Supporting Evidence (AHIDF Guidelines 2.0, Section 19): Implementation Mechanism – committees monitor projects; PMA facilitates implementation and coordination.  
- Supporting Evidence (AHIDF Guidelines 2.0, Section 20): Project Monitoring Unit handles progress reports, desk and field monitoring, and evaluation before PAC/PSC.  
- Supporting Evidence (Admin Approval of DIDF Scheme 2020-21): Objectives and components of DIDF explicitly listed for utilization tracking.
