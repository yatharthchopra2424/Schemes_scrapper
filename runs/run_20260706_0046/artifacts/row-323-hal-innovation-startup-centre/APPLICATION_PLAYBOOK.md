## Stage 1: Eligibility Verification (Day 1–3)

### Checklist
- [ ] Startup is registered as a legal entity in India (Private Limited, LLP, or recognized startup under DPIIT)
- [ ] Startup operates in aerospace, defence, avionics, materials, MRO, or related technological domains
- [ ] Startup's innovation aligns with HAL's technological requirements and Make-in-India initiatives
- [ ] Startup demonstrates potential for technology collaboration, pilot projects, or integration into HAL's supply chain
- [ ] Startup has a prototype or minimum viable product (MVP) ready for testing and validation
- [ ] Startup is willing to negotiate intellectual property terms on a case-by-case basis with HAL
- [ ] Startup understands that participation is subject to HAL's internal evaluation and alignment with its technological roadmap
- [ ] Startup acknowledges no guarantee of funding, order placement, or long-term collaboration

> **Note**: Eligibility is based on thematic alignment and technological readiness rather than quantitative thresholds (turnover, employee count, age of business). Verification focuses on domain relevance and innovation value proposition.

## Stage 2: Document Collection (Day 3–10)

### Universal Documents (always required)
- Certificate of Incorporation / Registration
- PAN Card of the entity
- GST Registration Certificate (if applicable)
- Udyam Registration Certificate
- Bank statements (last 6 months)
- Income Tax Returns (ITR) of the entity for last 2 financial years (if applicable)
- Audited financial statements (if available)
- Founder/ promoter KYC (Aadhaar, PAN, photograph)
- Board resolution authorizing application (for companies)
- Declaration of compliance with scheme guidelines

### Scheme-Specific Documents
- Detailed project proposal outlining the innovative technology/solution
- Technical description of the innovation (including diagrams, schematics, or prototypes)
- Evidence of alignment with HAL's technological domains (aerospace, defence, avionics, materials, MRO, etc.)
- Prototype/demo video or test results (if available)
- Intellectual property details (patents filed/granted, if any)
- Roadmap for potential collaboration with HAL (pilot, testing, integration)
- Make-in-India compliance declaration
- Any existing collaborations or partnerships with defence/aerospace entities

### Document Quality Check
- All documents must be legible, scanned in PDF format (max file size: 5 MB per document)
- File names must follow format: `SchemeName_DocumentType_ClientName_Date.pdf` (e.g., `HAL_Innovation_Startup_Centre_Proposal_ABC_Startups_20240515.pdf`)
- All certificates must be valid and not expired
- Technical documents must be in English or accompanied by certified English translation
- Declarations and undertakings must be signed and stamped (where applicable)
- Board resolution must be dated and specify authorized signatory

## Stage 3: Application Drafting (Day 10–18)

### Project Report / DPR (if required)
A formal Detailed Project Report (DPR) is **not explicitly required**, but a comprehensive **technical project proposal** is essential. This should include:
- Executive summary of the innovation
- Problem statement and proposed solution
- Technical architecture and working principle
- Current development stage (TRL level)
- Alignment with HAL’s technological domains
- Testing/validation requirements and how HAL facilities can support
- Potential for collaboration (pilot, integration, supply chain)
- IP status and proposed terms
- Roadmap for next 12–24 months
- Team qualifications and capabilities

> **Note**: Verify specific proposal format expectations with HAL’s nodal officer, as no standardized template is published.

### Application Form Filling Protocol
1. Access the HAL official website ([https://hal-india.co.in](https://hal-india.co.in)) and navigate to the “Innovation” or “Startups” section (if available).
2. If no online form exists, prepare a cover letter addressed to the Head of Innovation Centre, HAL, and submit via email or post as per instructions.
3. Clearly state the startup’s name, registration details, and point of contact in all correspondence.
4. Explicitly mention alignment with HAL’s technological requirements and Make-in-India initiative.
5. Avoid claiming any guaranteed financial support; focus on collaboration and testing opportunities.
6. Attach all scheme-specific documents as per the checklist.
7. Ensure the proposal is concise (max 5–10 pages) and technically rigorous.
8. Retain a copy of the submitted application and obtain proof of dispatch (email read receipt, courier acknowledgment, or portal screenshot).

### Internal Review Gate
- [ ] All universal and scheme-specific documents are collected and quality-checked
- [ ] Technical proposal clearly articulates innovation and HAL alignment
- [ ] No mention of guaranteed funding or orders (manages expectations)
- [ ] IP considerations are addressed proactively
- [ ] Application is addressed to the correct authority (Innovation Centre, HAL)
- [ ] Submission method (email/post/portal) is confirmed and documented
- [ ] All declarations are signed and dated
- [ ] Reference number or acknowledgment is requested upon submission
