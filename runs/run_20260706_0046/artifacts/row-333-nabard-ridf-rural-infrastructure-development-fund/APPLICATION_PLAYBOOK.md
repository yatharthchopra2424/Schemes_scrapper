## Stage 1: Eligibility Verification (Day 1–3)

### Checklist
- [ ] Applicant is a state government, state-owned corporation, local self-government, NGO, or cooperative  
- [ ] Project falls under the approved list of activities under RIDF (agriculture and allied sectors, irrigation, soil conservation, drinking water, rural roads, bridges, etc.)  
- [ ] Project is technically feasible, economically viable, and socially acceptable  
- [ ] Project is an ongoing project (not for new land acquisition)  
- [ ] State government or implementing agency contribution is arranged as per prescribed ratio (20% for general states, 5–15% for special category states)  
- [ ] No prior default on NABARD loans or utilization certificate delays  
- [ ] Project located within the geographic scope of Pan-India  

> **Sources**:  
> - Eligibility: State governments, state-owned corporations, local self-governments, NGOs, and cooperatives are eligible to avail loans under RIDF for rural infrastructure projects. Projects must be technically feasible, economically viable, and socially acceptable, and should fall under the approved list of activities under the fund.  
> - Key Caveats: Loan is available only for ongoing projects, not for new land acquisition; Projects must be from the approved list of activities under RIDF; State government contribution is mandatory as per prescribed ratio.  
> - Application Process: Submit application through the state government nodal officer to NABARD.

## Stage 2: Document Collection (Day 3–10)

### Universal Documents (always required)
- Incorporation/Registration Certificate  
- PAN Card of the applicant entity  
- Audited Financial Statements (last 3 years)  
- Income Tax Returns (ITR) of the applicant (last 3 years)  
- GST Registration Certificate (if applicable)  
- Bank Statements (last 6 months)  
- Udyam Registration (if applicable for MSMEs, though not primary for this scheme)  
- Board Resolution authorizing the application and signatory  

### Scheme-Specific Documents
- Detailed Project Report (DPR) including technical, financial, and economic aspects  
- Land ownership or lease documents  
- Clearances from relevant authorities (e.g., irrigation, forest, pollution, environment)  
- Financial viability and cost estimates  
- Implementation schedule (Gantt chart or timeline)  
- Undertaking from state government or implementing agency (on stamp paper)  
- Previous utilization certificates (for ongoing projects seeking additional funding)  
- Approval letter from state government nodal department (if applicable)  

### Document Quality Check
- All documents must be legible, and not exceed 5 MB individually  
- File names must follow format: `SchemeName_DocumentType_ApplicantName_Date.pdf` (e.g., `NABARD_RIDF_DPR_StateAgriDept_20240515.pdf`)  
- All certificates and clearances must be valid and not expired  
- Undertakings and government letters must be on official letterhead and duly signed/stamped  
- DPR must be signed by the project engineer and authorized signatory of the applicant  
- Utilization certificates must be in the format prescribed by NABARD and audited by a CA  
- Hindi or English language only; vernacular documents must be accompanied by certified translation

## Stage 3: Application Drafting (Day 10–18)

### Project Report / DPR (if required)
**Required**: Yes, a Detailed Project Report (DPR) is mandatory.  
**Standard Sections to Include**:  
1. Executive Summary  
2. Project Background and Justification  
3. Technical Description (design, drawings, specifications)  
4. Site Details and Land Information  
5. Implementation Schedule (month-wise timeline)  
6. Cost Estimates (item-wise, with quotes)  
7. Financial Viability Analysis (IRR, NPV, payback period, sensitivity analysis)  
8. Arrangement of Funds (loan vs. equity, NABARD share, state contribution)  
9. Implementation Arrangements (agency responsible, monitoring mechanism)  
10. Environmental and Social Impact Assessment (if applicable)  
11. Risk Assessment and Mitigation Plan  
12. Annexures: Clearances, land documents, quotes, charts  

> **Sources**:  
> - Required Documents: Detailed Project Report (DPR) including technical, financial, and economic aspects.  
> - Application Process: Prepare detailed project report (DPR) including technical, financial, and economic aspects.

### Application Form Filling Protocol
1. The application is **not submitted directly via a portal**; it must be routed through the **state government nodal officer** to NABARD.  
2. Obtain the official RIDF application format from the state nodal department or NABARD regional office.  
3. Fill all fields in block letters; avoid overwriting or use of correction fluid.  
4. Ensure the project title matches exactly across DPR, application form, and clearance documents.  
5. Clearly state the loan amount sought and the percentage of total project cost it represents.  
6. Declare the state government’s contribution amount and source (budget, loan, etc.).  
7. Attach a checklist of all enclosed documents as per NABARD’s prescribed list.  
8. Have the application signed by the authorized signatory and countersigned by the state nodal officer (where applicable).  
9. Retain a photocopy of the submitted application for office records.  
10. Note: Since the nabard.org/ridf page returned a 404, applicants must confirm the current submission protocol with the NABARD regional office or state nodal agency.  

> **Sources**:  
> - Application Process: Submit application through the state government nodal officer to NABARD.  
> - Supporting Evidence: URL https://nabard.org/ridf returned HTTP 404 – Not Found; therefore, portal-specific guidance is unavailable, and applicants must verify with nodal officer.

## Stage 4: Submission & Follow-Up (Day 18–30)

### Submission Protocol
- Submit the application package to the **designated state government nodal officer** (e.g., Secretary, Rural Development; or Engineer-in-Chief, Irrigation Department)  
- Obtain a signed acknowledgment receipt with date, inward number, and officer’s signature  
- Scan and store the acknowledgment receipt in the case folder  
- Note the expected forwarding timeline to NABARD (typically 7–15 days)  
- Set a follow-up reminder for **Day 25** to check status with the nodal officer  
- If no acknowledgment is received within 3 days, escalate to the nodal officer’s office

## Stage 5: Post-Approval (Day 30–90+)

### Approval Actions
- [ ] Obtain and sanction letter from NABARD specifying loan amount, interest rate, repayment tenor, and disbursement conditions  
- [ ] Verify sanctioned amount against requested amount and eligibility (80–95% of project cost)  
- [ ] Execute loan agreement with NABARD on non-judicial stamp paper  
- [ ] Submit first utilization certificate and progress report to trigger first disbursement  
- [ ] Ensure utilization certificates are submitted quarterly or as per NABARD’s prescribed schedule  
- [ ] Maintain separate bank account for RIDF funds (if required by NABARD)  
- [ ] Track disbursements against utilization certificates for audit readiness  
- [ ] Submit project completion report and final utilization certificate upon project end  
- [ ] Monitor repayment schedule and ensure timely debit from designated account  
- [ ] Conduct internal audit of fund utilization before submitting UC to NABARD  
- [ ] Prepare and submit invoice for success fee as per engagement letter with client  

> **Sources**:  
> - Financial Support: Funds are disbursed in installments based on project progress and utilization certificates.  
> - Key Caveats: Utilization certificates and progress reports are mandatory for subsequent disbursements.  
> - Application Process: Upon approval, loan agreement is executed. Funds are released in stages based on project progress and utilization certificates.  
> - Benefits: Long-term financing at concessional interest rates for rural infrastructure projects.
