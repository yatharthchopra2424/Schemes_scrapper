### Opening (2 min)
Good morning/afternoon. I specialise in helping state governments, NGOs, and cooperatives access long-term financing for rural infrastructure projects through NABARD’s Rural Infrastructure Development Fund (RIDF). Many organisations like yours are looking to upgrade irrigation, rural roads, or drinking water systems but face funding gaps. I’d like to understand your current rural infrastructure initiatives to see if RIDF’s concessional loans—covering up to 85-95% of project costs—could support your goals.

### Discovery Questions
- What type of rural infrastructure project are you currently planning or implementing (e.g., irrigation, rural roads, bridges, soil conservation, drinking water)?
- Is your organisation a state government entity, state-owned corporation, local self-government, NGO, or cooperative?
- Has the project been identified from NABARD’s approved list of RIDF activities?
- What stage is the project at—conceptual, ongoing, or completed—and have you incurred any expenses to date?
- Do you have land ownership or lease documents secured for the project site?
- Have you obtained necessary clearances from authorities such as irrigation, forest, or pollution control boards?
- What is your estimated project cost, and what portion are you seeking to finance through external loans?
- Are you prepared to provide the mandatory state government or implementing agency contribution as per RIDF guidelines?

### Qualification Checklist (internal — don't read aloud)
- [ ] Organisation type: State government, state-owned corporation, local self-government, NGO, or cooperative
- [ ] Project falls under approved RIDF activities (agriculture, irrigation, soil conservation, drinking water, rural roads, bridges, etc.)
- [ ] Project is technically feasible, economically viable, and socially acceptable
- [ ] Detailed Project Report (DPR) can be prepared or is available
- [ ] Land ownership or lease documents are available
- [ ] Clearances from relevant authorities (irrigation, forest, pollution) can be obtained
- [ ] Financial viability and cost estimates are documented
- [ ] Implementation schedule is defined
- [ ] Undertaking from state government or implementing agency can be provided
- [ ] Previous utilization certificates available (for ongoing projects)
- [ ] Willingness to meet state government contribution requirement (15-20% for general states, 5-10% for special category states)
- [ ] Commitment to submit progress reports and utilization certificates for staged disbursements

### Transition to Pitch
Based on your responses, your [project type] project appears aligned with NABARD RIDF’s eligibility criteria. RIDF offers concessional loans covering up to 85-95% of project costs for rural infrastructure—let me explain how this could reduce your funding burden and accelerate implementation.

## 2. Scheme Pitch (5 min)

### Structure: Problem → Scheme → Value → Proof → Next Step

**Problem framing:**
Many rural infrastructure projects face delays or scaling challenges due to insufficient upfront financing, especially when traditional bank credit is inadequate for long-term, high-impact initiatives in agriculture, irrigation, or rural connectivity.

**Scheme intro:**
NABARD Rural Infrastructure Development Fund (RIDF) is a loan scheme administered by the National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development (NABARD) that provides long-term financing at concessional interest rates to state governments, state-owned corporations, local self-governments, NGOs, and cooperatives for ongoing rural infrastructure projects. Loans cover up to 95% of project costs for special category states and 80-85% for general states, with funds disbursed in stages based on project progress.

- Your organisation is a [state government/NGO/cooperative/etc.], which is explicitly eligible under RIDF guidelines

- The project is ongoing or ready for implementation, meeting RIDF’s requirement that loans are not for new land acquisition but for completion of eligible infrastructure
