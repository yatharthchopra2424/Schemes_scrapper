## Scheme Deep Dive

### Overview
The **NABARD Rural Infrastructure Development Fund (RIDF)** is a long-term financing mechanism established by the National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development (NABARD) to provide concessional loans for rural infrastructure projects across India. It operates on a **rolling basis** with no fixed deadline, allowing proposals to be submitted throughout the year based on annual allocations. The fund targets **state governments, state-owned corporations, local self-governments, NGOs, and cooperatives** for projects in agriculture, irrigation, soil conservation, drinking water, rural roads, bridges, and other approved rural infrastructure activities.

### Objectives
- To provide loans to state governments and state-owned corporations for **ongoing rural infrastructure projects** (not new land acquisition).
- To support projects in **agriculture and allied sectors, irrigation, soil conservation, drinking water, rural roads, and bridges**.
- To encourage **private sector participation** in rural infrastructure through **public-private partnerships (PPPs)**.
- To augment resources for rural infrastructure where **normal bank credit flow is inadequate**.
- To promote **sustainable and inclusive rural development**, including employment generation and improved agricultural productivity.

### Eligibility Matrix
| Eligible Entity Type | Specific Requirements | Notes |
|----------------------|------------------------|-------|
| State Governments | Must be the nodal agency for project submission; must contribute mandatory share | Primary borrower |
| State-Owned Corporations | Must be government-owned; project must fall under approved RIDF activities | Eligible as implementing agency |
| Local Self-Governments (Panchayats, Municipalities) | Must have legal authority to undertake project; must provide state contribution | Eligible via state government routing |
| NGOs | Must be registered; must partner with state government for sponsorship; project must be on approved list | Requires state government undertaking |
| Cooperatives | Must be registered under relevant Coop Act; must have state government backing | Eligible for agri-infrastructure projects |

> **Key Eligibility Caveats**  
> - Projects must be **technically feasible, economically viable, and socially acceptable**.  
> - Only projects from the **approved list of RIDF activities** are eligible (e.g., irrigation, rural roads, drinking water supply, soil conservation, markets, storage).  
> - **Land acquisition is not eligible**; fund is for **ongoing projects only**.  
> - **State government contribution is mandatory** as per prescribed ratio (see Financial Support).  
> - NGOs and cooperatives **must route applications through state government nodal officers**.

### Application Process Steps (Textual Summary)
1. **Identify** an eligible rural infrastructure project from the approved RIDF activity list.
2. **Prepare** a Detailed Project Report (DPR) covering technical feasibility, financial viability, cost estimates, implementation schedule, and required clearances.
3. **Submit** the application through the **state government nodal officer** to NABARD (not directly).
4. **NABARD appraises** the project for technical, financial, and economic viability.
5. **Upon approval**, execute a formal **loan agreement** with NABARD.
6. **Disbursement** occurs in **stages** linked to project milestones, requiring submission of **utilization certificates** and progress reports for each tranche.
7. **Repeat** utilization certificate submission for subsequent disbursements until project completion.

### Required Documents
1. Detailed Project Report (DPR)
2. Land ownership or lease documents
3. Clearances from relevant authorities (irrigation, forest, pollution, etc.)
4. Financial viability and cost estimates
5. Implementation schedule
6. Undertaking from state government or implementing agency
7. Previous utilization certificates (for ongoing projects)
